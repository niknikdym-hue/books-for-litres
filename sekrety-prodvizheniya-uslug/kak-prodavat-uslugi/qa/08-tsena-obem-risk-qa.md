**PASS.**

## 3. Research integrity

**PASS WITH STRONG CONTEXT LIMITS.**

Traceability: `../source-notes/08-tsena-obem-risk-sources.md`.

Evidence functions:

- Alavi et al. (JAMS 2020) — service heterogeneity/inseparability can affect price-negotiation aspirations through risk/legitimacy/integration mechanisms;
- Mitchell/Moutinho/Lewis — financial risk and risk-reduction concepts in organisational professional-service purchasing;
- Homburg & Stebel — professional-service contract terms depend on service characteristics; performance-based contracts not automatically optimal even with measurable/verifiable output;
- Jørgensen et al. — fixed-price vs T&M software-project outcomes/behaviour differed in two studied data sets;
- Gopal / related outsourcing work — counter-context showing contract-incentive results are not one-directional;
- Huang et al. — monitoring/renegotiation as uncertainty-management mechanisms in software outsourcing;
- FTC / service-price research — price-quality relationship is not universal;
- service-guarantee research — guarantees can change perceived risk in specific service contexts, not universal professional-service outcome guarantee.

Not claimed:

- fixed price universally worse/better;
- T&M universally better;
- performance pricing universally aligned;
- high price proves quality;
- a guarantee always reduces risk;
- our seven-field map is a scientifically validated pricing framework.

1. work result / commitment;
2. defined vs expandable scope;
3. material uncertainty;
4. current risk bearer;
5. relation of payment/pricing model to risk;
6. real economic lever to change;

Possible decisions:

- change scope/work result;
- stage commitment;
- change risk allocation/pricing basis;
- change payment timing/milestones;
