# SOURCE NOTES — Глава 8 «Цена, объём и риск»

**Статус:** SOURCE NOTES / NOT MANUSCRIPT  
**Дата проверки:** 2026-09-07  
**Research question:** можно ли построить сильную главу о цене профессиональной услуги как о конструкции `price–scope–uncertainty–risk allocation`, не скатываясь в «продавайте ценность», high-price signaling или универсальный billing model?

## 1. Price negotiation for services is shaped by service uncertainty

### Alavi, Habel, Schwenke & Schmitz — Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 2020
`Price negotiating for services: elucidating the ambivalent effects on customers’ negotiation aspirations`  
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-019-00676-4

Research frame:
- five empirical studies including experiments and field data;
- almost 1,300 customers across studies;
- examines service vs goods price-negotiation behavior and service heterogeneity/inseparability.

Reported mechanisms relevant to Ch8:
- service heterogeneity can increase negotiation aspirations because of perceived product/quality risk and perceived legitimacy of negotiation;
- service inseparability/integration can produce the opposite effect because customers may fear negative consequences of negotiating;
- customization and customer integration matter as boundary conditions.

Use in Ch8:
- price negotiation around services cannot be reduced to `client wants cheaper`;
- uncertainty about service quality/benefits and the nature of co-production can influence how price negotiation is perceived;
- gives empirical basis for treating price tension partly as a risk/uncertainty issue.

Boundary:
- do NOT teach the paper’s influence/communication tactics as manipulation techniques;
- do NOT generalize exact effects to every professional service or Russian buyer;
- no claim that discounts should or should not be given based on one universal rule.

## 2. Financial risk is material in purchasing professional services

### Mitchell, Moutinho & Lewis — The Service Industries Journal, 2003
`Risk Reduction in Purchasing Organisational Professional Services`  
https://doi.org/10.1080/02642060308565621

Reported abstract findings:
- organisational professional services can be costly, time-consuming, high-profile and of difficult/variable quality;
- overall perceived risk in the studied high-risk scenario was driven mainly by financial risk;
- identified risk-reduction concepts included clarifying, simplifying and risk sharing.

Use in Ch8:
- supports financial risk as an independent buyer concern;
- supports transaction redesign/risk sharing as a legitimate response rather than pure persuasion/discounting.

Boundary:
- not-for-profit organisational purchasing context;
- no universal hierarchy of risk-reduction tactics;
- no direct prescription of a specific fee model.

Related background:
Mitchell 1998, `Buy-phase and buy-class effects on organisational risk perception and reduction in purchasing professional services`  
https://doi.org/10.1108/08858629810246779

## 3. Contract terms in professional services depend on service characteristics

### Homburg & Stebel — Management Accounting Research, 2009
`Determinants of contract terms for professional services`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mar.2008.10.001

Research context:
- empirical study of German management consulting firms;
- professional services characterized by transactional uncertainty and double moral-hazard risk;
- examines determinants of fixed-cost vs variable/performance-linked contract terms.

Reported findings:
1. service characteristics significantly affect contract type;
2. performance-based contracts may not be optimal even when service output is measurable/verifiable;
3. experience-based trust and reputation affect control choices in short-term contracts.

Use in Ch8:
- strong evidence against `performance pricing is always more aligned`;
- supports matching economic terms to service characteristics, uncertainty and co-production;
- client/provider both can affect service cost/output.

Boundary:
- management consulting/German sample;
- no universal pricing formula;
- no claim that one model maximizes margin or client value.

## 4. Fixed price vs time-and-materials evidence is context-dependent

### Jørgensen, Mohagheghi & Grimstad — International Journal of Project Management, 2017
`Direct and indirect connections between type of contract and software project outcome`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijproman.2017.09.003

Research frame:
- two empirical studies of outsourced software projects;
- one international small-project/task data set and one Norwegian public-client project data set.

Reported findings:
- fixed-price contracts were associated with higher failure risk than time-and-materials in both studies;
- differences were connected with provider selection, client involvement, agile practices and benefit management.

Use:
- demonstrates that pushing effort/estimate risk to provider through fixed price can change behavior/outcomes;
- useful for `fixed price for uncertain scope is not automatically safer overall`.

Boundary:
- software outsourcing context;
- association/empirical context must not be converted into universal `T&M is better` advice.

### Gopal & Sivaramakrishnan — Information Systems Research, 2008
`On Vendor Preferences for Contract Types in Offshore Software Projects: The Case of Fixed Price vs. Time and Materials Contracts`  
https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.1070.0162

Research frame:
- 93 offshore projects from one leading Indian vendor;
- vendor profitability/preferences differed by contractual regime and project contingencies.

Use:
- reinforces that provider incentives/preferences change with contract form and project conditions.

Boundary:
- single-vendor offshore-software context.

### Gopal — Decision Sciences, 2010
`The Role of Contracts on Quality and Returns to Quality in Offshore Software Development Outsourcing`  
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-5915.2010.00278.x

Study frame:
- 100 software projects from a leading Indian offshore vendor;
- reports different quality/incentive outcomes across FP vs T&M in that context.

Use:
- important counterweight: contract-form evidence is not one-directional across all studies/settings;
- strengthens chapter rule: **do not universalize contract type**.

## 5. Uncertainty can require monitoring/renegotiation rather than pretending scope is fixed

### Huang, Hu, Kauffman & Xu — Information Systems Research, 2021
`The Power of Renegotiation and Monitoring in Software Outsourcing: Substitutes or Complements?`  
https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2021.1026

Use:
- treats monitoring and renegotiation as responses to information asymmetry and uncertainty in outsourcing;
- supports explicit change/review rules as part of economic design when uncertainty cannot be eliminated at contracting.

Boundary:
- theoretical/analytical software-outsourcing context;
- no recommendation that all professional services should have renegotiation clauses.

## 6. Price is not a universally reliable quality signal

### Murphy — U.S. Federal Trade Commission working paper, 2002
`Price and Quality Relationships in Local Service Industries`  
https://www.ftc.gov/reports/price-quality-relationships-local-service-industries

Data frame:
- consumer price/quality data across 19 local service industries in Washington, D.C. area;
- only 3 of 19 industries showed consistently significant positive price-quality correlations in reported results; some repair industries showed negative correlations.

Use in Ch8:
- strong guardrail against `higher price proves higher quality`;
- price may function as a cue in some settings, but actual price-quality relationship is not universal.

Boundary:
- local U.S. consumer-service data; older;
- do not transfer specific correlations to Russia/professional B2B.

### Turley & Kelley — service price/quality experiment, 1995
`The Effects of Awareness and Quality Risk on the Price-Quality Relationship Among Services`  
https://doi.org/10.1300/J127v01n01_06

Reported result:
- price and awareness interacted in perceived service-quality evaluations;
- price-quality relationship appeared under some awareness conditions but not others.

Use:
- price-as-quality cue is context-dependent, not a law.

Boundary:
- old experimental study; supplementary only.

## 7. Service guarantees can affect risk, but are context-specific

### International Journal of Hospitality Management, 2012
`Service guarantees in the hotel industry: Their effects on consumer risk and service quality perceptions`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhm.2011.09.012

Study frame:
- experiment with 222 hotel consumers;
- guarantee type and corporate reputation affected perceived risk/quality in the studied setting.

Use:
- guarantees/commitments can alter perceived risk;
- chapter may discuss bounded risk reversal as one possible transaction-design lever.

Boundary:
- hotel consumer context;
- professional services with uncontrollable external outcomes are different;
- never recommend outcome guarantees beyond provider control;
- no blanket money-back guarantee recipe.

## 9. AI-era pricing is current context, not core law

### Brynjolfsson & Petropoulos — Stanford Digital Economy Lab working paper, 23 Aug 2026
`Pricing Consulting Services in the Age of Agentic AI: A Strategy Map for Executives`  
https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/pricing-consulting-services-in-the-age-of-agentic-ai-a-strategy-map-for-executives/

Current argument:
- agentic AI changes observability of inputs/outcomes and can alter fit of time-based, project, hybrid, subscription and outcome-based models;
- authors frame pricing choice through outcome observability and input-cost observability;
- suggests movement toward hybrid/asset-based structures in consulting as AI changes information environment.

Use in Ch8:
- current 2026 evidence that time-based professional-service pricing is under structural pressure when technology changes relation between effort, cost and client-observable output;
- supports including `what exactly is client paying for when effort falls?` as current discussion.

Boundary:
- working paper, consulting/executive context;
- not universal rule for all services;
- do not copy their strategy map or make AI a standalone chapter.

### Current journalism context (Sept 2026)
Financial Times / other reporting documents pressure from large clients on law/consulting fees and movement toward fixed/alternative arrangements as AI affects perceived effort economics.

Use only as time-sensitive illustration; no universal generalization to Russian SMEs.

## 10. Approved practical synthesis candidate

### Price–Scope–Risk Map / Карта «Цена — объём — риск»

Status: **AUTHOR-CREATED SYNTHESIS**, not a validated scientific pricing model.

For one real offer, reader maps:

1. **ОБЯЗАТЕЛЬСТВО / РЕЗУЛЬТАТ РАБОТЫ**  
   What exactly is being paid for? Use Ch2 result, not generic value claim.

2. **ОБЪЁМ / ЕДИНИЦА РАБОТЫ**  
   What is fixed/limited/measurable? What can expand?

3. **КЛЮЧЕВАЯ НЕОПРЕДЕЛЁННОСТЬ**  
   What remains uncertain in effort, scope, inputs, process or output at contract time?

4. **КТО СЕЙЧАС НЕСЁТ ЭТОТ РИСК**  
   Provider / client / shared? What happens if uncertainty resolves badly?

5. **КАК ТЕКУЩАЯ ЦЕНА/МОДЕЛЬ ОПЛАТЫ С ЭТИМ СВЯЗАНА**  
   Fixed fee / hourly-time / staged / hybrid / contingent component / other — descriptive, not ranked.

6. **ЧТО МОЖНО ИЗМЕНИТЬ БЕЗ ЛОЖНОЙ СКИДКИ**  
   Candidate levers:
   - scope/work-result reduction;
   - staged commitment / paid diagnostic / pilot where appropriate;
   - client-input requirement/change;
   - change/review/renegotiation point;
   - payment timing/milestones;
   - different pricing basis where justified;
   - bounded guarantee/risk-sharing only for controllable commitments.

7. **ЧТО ИЗМЕНЕНИЕ ЦЕНЫ НЕ ДОЛЖНО ЛОМАТЬ**  
   Quality floor, professional/ethical/legal boundary, explicit work-result integrity.

Decision outcomes:
- keep current economics;
- change scope/commitment;
- stage the uncertainty;
- change risk allocation/payment model;
- reduce price only together with a real economic change or conscious margin decision;
- decline if buyer requires an economically/professionally impossible configuration.

## 11. Protected boundaries

### vs Ch6
Ch6: `is this configuration workable enough to sell?`  
Ch8: `how should economic terms allocate uncertainty/risk inside a workable deal?`

Ch8 does not re-qualify client/capacity from scratch.

### vs Ch7
Ch7: price/terms must be visible in the decision document.  
Ch8: why those terms have this structure and what to redesign under price tension.

### vs Ch9
Ch8 handles confirmed **economic/financial tension**.  
Ch9 handles ambiguous surface objections where cause is not yet known. It may route a confirmed price problem back to Ch8 but cannot reteach economics.

## 12. Anti-patterns

Do not teach:
- `higher price = higher quality`;
- `never discount`;
- `always value price`;
- `hourly billing is obsolete`;
- `fixed fee is always safer for buyer`;
- `performance pricing aligns everyone`;
- `three packages / anchoring` as universal solution;
- arbitrary markup/margin numbers without business data;
- fake scarcity;
- outcome guarantees beyond provider control;
- «charge what you’re worth» psychology;
- price objections as self-esteem problem;
- pricing based on seller confidence;
- copying Stanford AI pricing map.

## 13. Research decision

**PASS FOR CHAPTER ADMISSION WITH HIGH BOUNDARY DISCIPLINE.**

Evidence supports:
- service price negotiation interacts with perceived risk/heterogeneity/integration;
- financial risk is material in professional-service purchasing;
- contract/pricing form allocates incentives and risk differently;
- no one contract form is universally optimal;
- performance-based arrangements are not universally superior;
- price is not a universally reliable quality signal;
- risk sharing/clarification/staging/change rules can be legitimate transaction-design tools;
- AI is currently changing the effort/value/observability assumptions behind professional-service pricing.

Evidence does NOT support:
- one universal pricing model;
- our seven-field map as a validated scientific framework;
- universal margin/discount percentages;
- universal price-quality inference;
- universal transfer of software/consulting findings to every Russian service.
