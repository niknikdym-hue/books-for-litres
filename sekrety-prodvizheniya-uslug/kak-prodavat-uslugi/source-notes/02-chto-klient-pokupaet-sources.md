## 1. Mental intangibility

### Laroche et al. — Journal of Service Research, 2004
`Exploring How Intangibility Affects Perceived Risk`  
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1094670503262955

Use:
- intangibility is multidimensional;
- mental intangibility and generality showed stronger relationships with perceived-risk dimensions than physical intangibility in the study;
- supports chapter thesis that the buyer’s inability to mentally represent an offering matters commercially.

Do NOT claim:
- exact universal effect size;
- that every service buyer behaves identically;
- that mental tangibility alone solves purchase risk.

### Laroche, Bergeron & Goutaland — Journal of Services Marketing, 2003
`How intangibility affects perceived risk: the moderating role of knowledge and involvement`  
https://doi.org/10.1108/08876040310467907

Use:
- mental dimension accounted for more variance in perceived risk than the other intangibility dimensions in the reported model;
- supports the practical need to help a buyer form a clear mental representation of an offering.

## 2. Service specification uncertainty

### Mota & Santos — Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 2021
`Dynamic roles of suppliers in the specification of business services`  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0040162520313494

Use:
- customers may have limited knowledge for specifying complex business services;
- service specification can evolve in supplier–customer interaction;
- uncertainty about specification can be explicit rather than a hidden sales failure.

Boundary:
- longitudinal study context; do not universalize all findings to every B2C service;
- Ch2 uses it to justify clarity and provisional specification, not to teach discovery conversation (Ch5 territory).

## 3. Customer participation / co-production

### Dong et al. — Journal of Service Research, 2015
`Effect of Customer Participation on Service Outcomes: The Moderating Role of Participation Readiness`  
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1094670514551727

Use:
- participation is not automatically beneficial;
- participation readiness (ability, perceived benefit, role identification) changes the effect on service outcomes;
- supports explicit client-role design without shifting all accountability to the client.

Boundary:
- do not claim «the more the client participates, the better»;
- do not use as support for a generic client-responsibility disclaimer.

### Cermak, File & Prince — Journal of Applied Business Research, 1994
`Customer Participation In Service Specification And Delivery`  
https://clutejournals.com/index.php/JABR/article/view/5942

Use:
- professional-service customers can participate in specification and delivery;
- customer participation is a manageable service variable.

Boundary:
- older evidence; use as background, not sole modern support.
