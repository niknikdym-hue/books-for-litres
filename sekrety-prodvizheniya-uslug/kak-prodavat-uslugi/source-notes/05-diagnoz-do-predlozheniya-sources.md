## 1. Customer requirements definition is a distinct solution process

### Tuli, Kohli & Bharadwaj — Journal of Marketing, 2007
`Rethinking Customer Solutions: From Product Bundles to Relational Processes`  
https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.71.3.001

Study frame:
- depth interviews with 49 managers in customer firms;
- 55 managers in supplier firms;
- two focus groups with 21 managers;
- customer-side view of a solution differs from supplier bundle view.

Key research function for Ch5:
- customers described solutions as relational processes;
- **customer requirements definition** appears as a distinct process before customization/integration, deployment and support;
- supports the claim that defining the requirement/problem is not merely a preamble to the «real» solution.

Boundary:
- B2B solution context;
- does not validate our question sequence;
- does not imply every stated request is wrong;
- does not prove a conversion effect from asking more questions.

## 2. Professional services diagnosis can extend into pre-selling

### Sieg, Fischer, Wallin & von Krogh — Journal of Service Management, 2012
`Proactive diagnosis: how professional service firms sustain client dialogue`  
https://doi.org/10.1108/09564231211226132

Study frame:
- extensive field research with a Big Four accounting firm and 11 client companies;
- framework grounded in 49 focused interviews with professionals/client managers plus supplementary interviews, observations and firm documents.

Key research functions:
- diagnosis in professional services can extend beyond paid assignments into the pre-selling phase;
- proactive diagnosis includes **information-seeking** and **influence** strategies;
- professionals must manage a trade-off between these components and address client concerns.

Critical chapter boundary:
- «diagnosis» is not permission to manufacture a problem or force a preferred solution;
- influence exists in the phenomenon studied, therefore Ch5 must explicitly protect client agency and distinguish exploration from seller-led reframing;
- use information-seeking as the safer core of the practical artifact;
- if professional reframing is discussed, it must be testable against client evidence/concerns, not asserted from seller authority.

## 3. Value-based selling requires understanding customer context

### Terho, Haas, Eggert & Ulaga — Industrial Marketing Management, 2012
`“It’s almost like taking the sales out of selling”—Towards a conceptualization of value-based selling in business markets`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indmarman.2011.11.011

Study frame:
- discovery-oriented qualitative work based on in-depth interviews with sales managers across industries.

Three salient dimensions identified by the authors:
1. understanding the customer’s business model;
2. crafting the value proposition;
3. communicating customer value.

Use in Ch5:
- supports the separation of **understanding customer context before constructing the offer/value proposition**;
- reinforces that diagnosis is not merely objection handling or presentation technique.

Boundary:
- business-market context;
- not a universal consumer-service rule;
- do not import the authors’ dimensions as our chapter framework.

## 4. Problem formulation itself is vulnerable to bias

### Thomas & Schwenk — Interfaces / INFORMS, 1983
`Problem Formulation and the Consultant-Client Relationship`  
https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.13.5.25

Use:
- problem formulation is itself a decision process subject to biases;
- the paper discusses decision aids intended to facilitate problem formulation and the consultant-client relationship.

Chapter implication:
- the client’s first wording is data, not automatically the complete problem;
- equally, the consultant’s first interpretation is not automatically superior;
- Ch5 needs a visible `KNOWN / ASSUMED / UNKNOWN` discipline to prevent premature certainty on either side.

Boundary:
- older OR/consulting literature;
- background support only, not a modern sales-effectiveness trial.

## 5. SPIN Selling — benchmark boundary, not copied evidence framework

Architecture benchmark already reserves SPIN only as a quality/research-discipline comparator.

Ch5 must NOT reproduce:
- Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff sequence;
- renamed versions of those four question classes;
- scripted question count;
- claims that one universal sequence maximizes conversion.

Our chapter’s distinct object:
**information sufficiency before proposal**.

The practical decision is not «which question do I ask next to advance the sale?» but:

> Do we understand the situation, desired change, constraints, success criteria, decision context and remaining unknowns well enough to design a relevant proposal?

## 6. Approved chapter synthesis candidate

### Diagnostic Conversation Map / Карта диагностического разговора

Proposed fields:

1. **ИСХОДНАЯ СИТУАЦИЯ** — what is happening now, observable facts vs interpretation;
2. **ИЗМЕНЕНИЕ / РЕШЕНИЕ** — what the client needs to become different and why the issue matters now;
3. **ОГРАНИЧЕНИЯ** — budget/resource/time/technical/legal/organizational conditions that materially constrain the solution;
4. **КРИТЕРИИ ДОСТАТОЧНОГО РЕЗУЛЬТАТА** — what will count as useful/successful enough for this decision;
5. **КОНТЕКСТ РЕШЕНИЯ** — who uses, influences, approves or can block the decision where materially relevant;
6. **ИЗВЕСТНО / ПРЕДПОЛАГАЕМ / НЕИЗВЕСТНО** — explicit epistemic status of key inputs;
7. **ЧТО НУЖНО ПРОВЕРИТЬ ДО ПРЕДЛОЖЕНИЯ** — remaining evidence/information gap.

Status: **AUTHOR-CREATED SYNTHESIS**.

This is not a validated scientific scale and must never be described as one.

## 7. Why this does not duplicate other chapters

### Ch1
Ch1 diagnoses buyer-side purchase uncertainty. Ch5 investigates the **client’s actual task/context before solution design**.

### Ch2
Ch2 defines the service object once task territory is known. Ch5 determines whether enough is known to select/design that object for this client.

### Ch4
Ch4 visualizes future delivery after a service construction exists. Ch5 happens before offer design.

### Ch6
Ch5 answer: `what is the task / what do we still not know?`  
Ch6 answer: `given what we know, should this provider/client/scope/participation configuration proceed?`

The two chapters must remain separate in action and artifact. Ch6 retains hard merge-test if this separation fails in actual prose.

## 8. Ethics / anti-manipulation boundary

Chapter must reject these patterns:
- asking questions only to lead the client to a preselected product;
- inflating consequences to create urgency;
- pretending the seller already knows the hidden problem;
- treating every client answer as an objection to be overcome;
- diagnosing outside professional competence;
- gathering information that does not materially affect solution/fit/decision.

A diagnostic conversation may legitimately conclude:
- information is sufficient for an offer;
- more information is needed;
- the initial request should be reframed together with the client;
- the provider does not yet know enough;
- no relevant offer should be made.

## 9. Russia application

No unsupported «Russian buyer psychology» claims.

Model contexts:
- self-employed expert selling to an individual;
- owner-led small-business B2B;
- multi-stakeholder B2B where user, payer and approver differ.

Russia-specific legal/regulatory details appear only if they materially change what can be asked/promised in a regulated profession and require fresh official-source verification.

## 10. Research decision

**PASS FOR CHAPTER ADMISSION WITH BOUNDARIES.**

Evidence supports:
- requirements definition as meaningful work;
- pre-selling diagnosis in professional services;
- understanding customer context before value proposition;
- problem formulation as a potentially biased process.

Evidence does NOT support:
- a universal high-conversion script;
- the exact seven-field map as a validated scale;
- manipulative problem creation;
- universal transfer of B2B findings to every B2C service.
