# SOURCE NOTES — Глава 10 «Почему вы проиграли сделку — и что менять на самом деле»

**Статус:** SOURCE NOTES / NOT MANUSCRIPT  
**Дата проверки:** 2026-09-07  
**Research question:** достаточно ли evidence, чтобы завершить книгу post-outcome learning loop, который отделяет seller attribution от buyer evidence и не создаёт fake statistical precision для малого service business?

## 1. Buyer-side research directly identifies attribution-bias problem

### Friend, Curasi, Boles & Bellenger — Industrial Marketing Management, 2014
`Why are you really losing sales opportunities? A buyers' perspective on the determinants of key account sales failures`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indmarman.2014.06.002

Study frame:
- 35 semi-structured interview cases;
- industrial buying decision-makers interviewed following failed key-account sales proposals;
- naturalistic buyer-side post-mortem approach.

Important methodological point stated by authors:
- prior sales-failure work relying on salesperson/sales-manager/selling-firm data can introduce attribution biases;
- buyer perspective was used to reduce seller-side attribution problem.

Reported buyer-side themes:
- non-adaptive proposals;
- weak relationship potential;
- excessive cost considerations;
- each contained more complex sub-themes.

Use in Ch10:
- strong evidence that `CRM reason / seller story` is not equivalent to buyer-side cause;
- directly supports collecting buyer evidence after outcome where feasible;
- supports sales failure as multi-dimensional/process phenomenon rather than one label.

Boundary:
- key-account industrial B2B context;
- 35 qualitative cases, not incidence/frequency study;
- do NOT import three themes as our universal loss taxonomy;
- do NOT claim buyer interview always reveals objective truth; it adds an essential perspective, not omniscience.

## 2. Salespeople themselves can make attribution errors

### European Journal of Marketing, 2020/2021
`Impacts of salespeople’s biased and unbiased performance attributions on job satisfaction: the concept of misattributed satisfaction`  
https://doi.org/10.1108/EJM-11-2018-0816

Study frame:
- pre-test + between-subjects experiment;
- data from 209 salespeople;
- reports perceptual attribution errors in salespeople’s performance appraisal.

Use in Ch10:
- supports explicit caution that seller’s causal interpretation of an outcome can be biased even without dishonesty;
- reinforces need to mark `seller inference` separately from observed fact/buyer feedback.

Boundary:
- performance appraisal/job-satisfaction context, not individual deal win/loss coding;
- supplementary support only.

## 3. High-impact losses can encourage external attributions

### Mallin & Mayo — Journal of Personal Selling & Sales Management, 2006
`Why Did I Lose? A Conservation of Resources View of Salesperson Failure Attributions`  
https://doi.org/10.2753/PSS0885-3134260402

Reported finding in abstract:
- partial support that salespeople use external attributions following losses perceived as having high impact.

Use in Ch10:
- useful guardrail against comfortable external stories `budget / bad client / competitor / market` after painful loss;
- not proof that all salespeople externalize every loss.

Boundary:
- do not moralize; attribution can be protective/human rather than dishonest.

## 4. Win/loss practice can gather buyer voice, but practical guidance is expert practice

### Gartner case study, 18 July 2024
`Uncover Buying Decision Drivers With Win/Loss Analysis`  
https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/5597559

Use:
- current professional practice example of structured buyer interviews/surveys to understand buying decision drivers;
- shows win/loss analysis as an established B2B revenue practice.

Boundary:
- proprietary case study; no universal effectiveness statistic used.

### SurveyMonkey 2026 guide
https://www.surveymonkey.com/learn/market-research/win-loss-analysis/

Useful practice frame:
- outcome is signal;
- buyer voice adds evidence;
- repeated themes across multiple decisions can inform action;
- one deal remains anecdotal/directional.

Boundary:
- commercial guidance, not peer-reviewed science;
- do not copy sample-size thresholds or present them as statistical law.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy for small-service practice

Ch10 should explicitly distinguish:

### Level A — FACT OF OUTCOME
Examples:
- buyer signed / did not sign;
- date;
- amount/offer version;
- which proposal was sent;
- known follow-up events;
- explicit buyer statement actually received.

### Level B — SELLER INFERENCE
Examples:
- `проиграли из-за цены`;
- `не хватило доверия`;
- `долго отвечали`;
- `конкурент был сильнее`.

Must be labelled as hypothesis unless supported.

### Level C — BUYER FEEDBACK
Direct buyer account from:
- short post-decision question;
- interview;
- explicit email/message;
- procurement debrief where available.

Still perspective, not omniscient causal truth.

### Level D — CROSS-DEAL REPEAT
Same decision-relevant issue appears independently across several outcomes or source types.

Do not assign one universal minimum n for small practice. Mark strength:
- `ONE CASE / ANECDOTAL`;
- `REPEATED SIGNAL`;
- `SUPPORTED PATTERN` (requires multiple independent cases/evidence, but not automatically statistically representative).

### Level E — TEST
Choose one change with observable consequence in future comparable deals.

## 6. Approved practical synthesis

### Lost-Deal Learning Loop / Цикл обучения на сделках

Status: **AUTHOR-CREATED SYNTHESIS**, not validated statistical method.

For each recent outcome:

1. **ИСХОД / ФАКТЫ** — what objectively happened;
2. **ЧТО МЫ ДУМАЕМ, ЧТО ПРОИЗОШЛО** — seller explanation, clearly marked hypothesis;
3. **ЧТО СКАЗАЛ / ПОКАЗАЛ ПОКУПАТЕЛЬ** — direct feedback/evidence if available;
4. **КАКОЙ ЭТАП/АРТЕФАКТ КНИГИ МОГ БЫТЬ СВЯЗАН** — Ch1–9 only after evidence, not automatic blame;
5. **СИЛА СИГНАЛА** — one case / repeated / supported pattern;
6. **КАКОЕ ОДНО ИЗМЕНЕНИЕ СТОИТ ПРОВЕРИТЬ**;
7. **КАК УВИДИМ, ЧТО ИЗМЕНЕНИЕ ПОМОГЛО** — future observable check.

For a solo/small-business review, 5–10 recent outcomes can be a practical starting set, but **NOT a statistically valid sample by default**. Avoid numerical significance language.

## 7. Buyer feedback ethics / practicality

Not every lost buyer owes an interview.

Acceptable lightweight asks, where relationship/context permit:
- `Если вам удобно, назовите один фактор, который сильнее всего повлиял на выбор.`
- `Что в нашем предложении было менее убедительным/подходящим, чем в выбранном варианте?`
- `Если решение отложено, что изменилось между началом выбора и текущим моментом?`

Do not:
- pressure for explanation after clear no;
- dispute buyer feedback;
- turn feedback request into another sales attempt;
- ask leading `это из-за цены?` question;
- offer reward in a way that distorts or manipulates the account without disclosure/appropriateness.

## 8. Wins matter too

Do not analyze only losses.

Reason:
- a winning outcome can reinforce false story (`мы выиграли из-за кейса`) just as easily;
- comparing wins/losses/no-decision can help detect whether a factor actually varies with outcome;
- prevent changing a strong element because of one loss.

But do not claim causal inference from informal small samples.

## 9. System assembly — not a new eleventh framework

Final chapter may show the book’s operating loop:

`Ch1 diagnose purchase uncertainty`  
→ `Ch2 define the service decision object`  
→ `Ch3 make relevant proof visible`  
→ `Ch4 make future delivery observable`  
→ `Ch5 establish information sufficiency`  
→ `Ch6 check service compatibility`  
→ `Ch7 build decision-complete proposal`  
→ `Ch8 design economics/risk`  
→ `Ch9 diagnose live stall`  
→ `Ch10 learn from outcome`  
→ update **only the weak relevant component**, not rerun every tool.

This is **assembly of T01–T10**, not a separate Personal Service Sales System framework with new fields.

## 10. Russia application

Designed for:
- self-employed/NPD specialist with spreadsheet/notes;
- ИП/small agency with CRM or simple table;
- B2B team where buyer debrief may be available.

No BI/CRM implementation required.

Data/privacy specifics are not core; if book recommends storing personal data, recordings or automated call analysis in a concrete way, Russian legal/privacy requirements need a fresh official check. Avoid such detail in core.

## 11. Anti-patterns

Do not teach:
- `каждой проигранной сделке обязательно назначьте одну причину`;
- mandatory CRM loss code as truth;
- fake significance from 5–10 deals;
- percentage dashboards without denominator/context;
- `buyer feedback is objective truth`;
- only-loss analysis;
- postmortem as sales retry;
- AI automatic call analysis as substitute for buyer evidence;
- giant BI system;
- reopening every prior chapter tool after each loss;
- convenient blame categories (`price / bad lead / competitor`) without evidence.

## 12. Research decision

**PASS FOR CHAPTER ADMISSION.**

Evidence supports:
- seller-side attribution bias is a legitimate problem;
- buyer-side post-mortem evidence can reveal richer/multi-dimensional causes of sales failure;
- salespeople can make biased causal attributions without dishonesty;
- win/loss analysis is established professional practice.

Evidence does NOT support:
- our exact loop as validated causal/statistical method;
- a universal loss-reason taxonomy;
- fixed sample-size threshold for small practice;
- buyer account as perfect objective truth;
- causal conclusions from informal 5–10-deal review.
