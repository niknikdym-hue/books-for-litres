# SOURCE NOTES — Глава 7 «Коммерческое предложение как документ решения»

**Статус:** SOURCE NOTES / NOT MANUSCRIPT  
**Дата проверки:** 2026-09-07  
**Research question:** есть ли достаточная evidence base, чтобы рассматривать коммерческое предложение как decision aid / integration layer, а не как красивый seller deck или универсальный шаблон?

## 1. Buyers of professional services evaluate multiple qualities ex ante

### Pemer & Skjølsvik — Journal of Business Research, 2019
`The cues that matter: Screening for quality signals in the ex ante phase of buying professional services`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2019.02.005

Study frame:
- qualitative interviews with 51 clients of professional services;
- focuses on how professional-service quality is assessed before purchase;
- develops dimensions/signals used in ex-ante screening.

Use in Ch7:
- professional-service buyers do not evaluate a proposal only as a price quote;
- proposal may need to make decision-relevant information about provider/service/delivery legible enough for ex-ante evaluation;
- supports integration of already-established proof and delivery information into the buyer’s decision object.

Boundary:
- management-consulting/professional-service context;
- Ch7 must not reteach Ch3’s signal taxonomy;
- no claim that a specific proposal layout increases conversion.

## 2. Final selection of consultants is multi-criteria

### Dawes, Dowling & Patterson — Industrial Marketing Management, 1992
`Criteria used to select management consultants`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/0019-8501(92)90015-L

Study frame:
- survey of 253 organizations;
- 17 choice criteria in final selection of management consultancy services;
- reputation and industry experience reported as top criteria in that sample.

Use in Ch7:
- selection is multi-criteria rather than price-only;
- proposal should make materially relevant decision criteria visible when they actually matter.

Boundary:
- old management-consultancy context;
- no universal ranking of criteria;
- do not copy the 17 criteria into our proposal artifact;
- Ch3 owns proof relevance, not Ch7.

### Patterson — Journal of Professional Services Marketing, 1995
`Choice Criteria in Final Selection of a Management Consultancy Service`  
https://doi.org/10.1300/J090v11n02_13

Study frame:
- survey of 142 client organizations;
- reputation, industry experience and cost among the most important criteria reported;
- differences by client familiarity/experience.

Use:
- supplementary evidence that different client contexts may weight selection information differently;
- reinforces no universal proposal order/ranking.

## 3. Tender/contractor evaluation is a multi-attribute decision

### Watt, Kayis & Willey — International Journal of Project Management, 2009
`Identifying key factors in the evaluation of tenders for projects and services`  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijproman.2008.03.002

Use:
- tender/contractor selection described as complex multi-attribute decision;
- reported principal criteria include management/technical capability, experience/performance, reputation and proposed delivery/technical solution.

Boundary:
- tender/project procurement context, not generic small-service proposal;
- do not convert tender criteria into universal КП sections;
- useful mainly to support multi-attribute nature of structured selection.

### ASCE Journal of Architectural Engineering, 2019
`Procurement of Architecture and Engineering Services: Influence of Cost on Selection Outcomes and Evaluation Criteria That Best Differentiate Consultant Expertise`
https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)AE.1943-5568.0000344

Study frame:
- proposal evaluation data set reported as n=804 consultant proposals in architecture/engineering procurement;
- analyzed criteria differentiating competing proposals and relationship of cost to selection.

Use:
- proposal documents are evaluated as bundles of differentiated criteria in real professional procurement contexts.

Boundary:
- qualification-based architecture/engineering procurement; do not transfer weighting/selection statistics to ordinary Russian professional services.

## 4. More information can help and hurt decision quality

### Keller & Staelin — Journal of Consumer Research, 1987
`Effects of Quality and Quantity of Information on Decision Effectiveness`  
https://doi.org/10.1086/209106

Experimental finding reported in abstract:
- attribute information can both help and hinder evaluation;
- information quality and quantity have distinguishable effects;
- more information quantity is not automatically more effective.

Use in Ch7:
- supports explicit rejection of `more pages / more proof / more detail = better proposal`;
- proposal should prioritize decision-relevant information, not maximize volume.

Boundary:
- consumer/product experimental context;
- no direct claim about proposal length or service-sales conversion.

## 5. Information organization changes decision process

### Schkade & Kleinmuntz — Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 1994
`Information Displays and Choice Processes: Differential Effects of Organization, Form, and Sequence`  
https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1994.1018

Study frame:
- 60 MBA students chose among loan applications described on multiple attributes;
- organization, form and sequence of information influenced choice processes/effort in different ways.

Use in Ch7:
- supports the general proposition that how decision-relevant information is organized changes information acquisition/evaluation effort;
- gives grounds for proposal-as-decision-aid framing.

Boundary:
- loan-application experimental context;
- no universal section order/layout;
- no claim that our Offer Decision Sheet is scientifically validated.

Related conceptual review:
Kleinmuntz & Schkade, `Information Displays and Decision Processes`, Psychological Science, 1993  
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00265.x

## 6. Approved synthesis candidate

### Offer Decision Sheet / Карта решения по предложению

This is an **AUTHOR-CREATED SYNTHESIS / audit tool**, not a mandatory proposal template.

Proposed decision fields:

1. **СИТУАЦИЯ / ЗАДАЧА**  
   What client situation is this proposal responding to? Uses Ch5 output without reteaching diagnosis.

2. **ЧТО ПРЕДЛАГАЕТСЯ КУПИТЬ**  
   Work result / service decision object, scope boundaries, important exclusions/assumptions. Uses Ch2.

3. **ПОЧЕМУ ЕСТЬ ОСНОВАНИЯ ВЫБРАТЬ ЭТОГО ИСПОЛНИТЕЛЯ**  
   Only the relevant proof needed for this decision. Uses Ch3; no proof catalogue.

4. **КАК БУДЕТ УСТРОЕНА РАБОТА**  
   Only decision-relevant stages, client role, checkpoints/change rules. Uses Ch4; no PM manual.

5. **КРИТИЧЕСКИЕ УСЛОВИЯ СОВМЕСТИМОСТИ**  
   Only conditions materially required for delivery after Ch6; no re-qualification questionnaire.

6. **ИНВЕСТИЦИЯ / УСЛОВИЯ ОПЛАТЫ**  
   Amount/terms must be visible. Economic design/reasoning belongs to Ch8.

7. **ЧТО ОСТАЁТСЯ НЕ ГАРАНТИРОВАНО / ЗАВИСИТ ОТ ВНЕШНИХ ФАКТОРОВ**  
   Residual risk/limits where materially relevant; no legalistic disclaimer dump.

8. **СЛЕДУЮЩЕЕ РЕШЕНИЕ / ДЕЙСТВИЕ**  
   What buyer needs to decide/do next; no fake urgency.

No universal mandatory order. The tool audits completeness/decision function, while actual proposal form/sequence can vary by context.

## 7. Observable buyer-retell test

Give the proposal to a relevant person who did not attend the sales conversation. Without additional explanation, ask them to state:

- what problem/situation this offer addresses;
- what exactly is being purchased;
- what material scope/boundaries exist;
- why this provider is plausibly capable;
- how the work will unfold at a meaningful level;
- what the buyer/client must do;
- how much/under what payment terms;
- what is not guaranteed / what depends on conditions;
- what decision/action is required next.

If the reader must call the seller to reconstruct a basic field, proposal has a decision-information gap.

This is an **author-created practical test**, not validated scale.

## 8. No duplication with earlier chapters

Ch2 → defines the service object. Ch7 only carries the result into proposal.

Ch3 → determines what evidence is relevant/verifiable. Ch7 only selects/places material proof.

Ch4 → designs Process Visibility Map. Ch7 only shows decision-relevant subset.

Ch5 → diagnosis / information sufficiency before offer. Ch7 consumes approved understanding; no discovery questions.

Ch6 → compatibility decision. Ch7 may state material conditions that survived qualification; no re-scoring.

## 9. Protected boundary with Chapter 8

Ch7 owns **visibility/integration of economic terms**.

Ch8 owns:
- why price tension arises;
- relation between price, scope, uncertainty and risk allocation;
- payment/staging/commitment redesign;
- what changes when price changes.

Ch7 must not teach discounts, packages, anchoring, value pricing or payment redesign.

## 10. Russia application

Distinguish:

### Owner-led / ordinary B2B
A proposal may be a short commercial document/email/table if decision is simple.

### More formal B2B
Buyer may require prescribed procurement/tender fields; our sheet works as internal decision-completeness audit, not replacement for procurement rules.

### B2C / individual services
Not every service needs a formal КП. Decision information may be distributed across website, offer/contract, message, booking flow. Any statutory pre-contract information requirements must be fresh-checked from official Russian sources.

Do not claim КП itself is a legal contract.

## 11. Anti-patterns

Do not teach:
- ideal number of pages;
- ideal visual design;
- mandatory three packages;
- price ending tricks;
- fake scarcity/deadlines;
- «about us» first because seller wants it;
- testimonial dump;
- all previous chapter tools pasted into one giant document;
- legal disclaimer wall;
- universal section order;
- invented conversion statistics.

## 12. Research decision

**PASS FOR CHAPTER ADMISSION WITH DECISION-AID BOUNDARY.**

Evidence is sufficient to support:
- ex-ante professional-service evaluation is multi-dimensional;
- selection can be multi-criteria;
- information quantity is not automatically beneficial;
- organization/display affects choice process and effort;
- proposal can reasonably be designed as a decision-support integration layer.

Evidence does NOT validate:
- our exact eight fields as scientific scale;
- one universal proposal structure;
- a specific page count/design/order;
- a conversion-rate claim.
