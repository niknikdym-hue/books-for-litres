# SOURCE NOTES — Глава 9 «„Я подумаю“ — это не одно возражение»

**Статус:** SOURCE NOTES / NOT MANUSCRIPT  
**Дата проверки:** 2026-09-07  
**Research question:** есть ли достаточно evidence, чтобы строить post-offer hesitation chapter как competing-hypothesis diagnosis, не выдавая одну objection taxonomy за научный закон и не повторяя Ch1?

## 1. Decision avoidance has multiple forms and antecedents

### Anderson — Psychological Bulletin, 2003
`The Psychology of Doing Nothing: Forms of Decision Avoidance Result From Reason and Emotion`  
https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.129.1.139

Review scope:
- integrates multiple independent research lines on postponement, inaction and status quo;
- identifies four decision-avoidance effects: choice deferral, status quo bias, omission bias, inaction inertia;
- review discusses antecedents including cost–benefit considerations, anticipated regret, selection difficulty and other rational/emotional components.

Use in Ch9:
- strong support for the chapter’s basic premise that **inaction/deferral is not one psychological state**;
- seller cannot infer one cause from the mere fact that buyer delayed or did nothing.

Boundary:
- not service-sales research;
- no direct mapping from the review’s four effects to our exact diagnostic categories;
- no claim that every stalled service purchase is irrational bias.

## 2. Status quo is a real alternative, but not a universal diagnosis

### Samuelson & Zeckhauser — Journal of Risk and Uncertainty, 1988
`Status Quo Bias in Decision Making`  
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00055564

Research frame:
- experiments plus real decision data from health-plan and retirement-program selections;
- reported disproportionate tendency to maintain current/default state.

Use in Ch9:
- supports `do nothing / keep current state` as a genuine decision alternative;
- helps distinguish timing/status-quo cause from provider/price/evidence cause.

Boundary:
- do not label every `не сейчас` as status quo bias;
- buyer may rationally choose current state because switching cost, priority, cash flow, uncertainty or alternative use of resources makes it optimal.

## 3. Preference uncertainty can lead to choice deferral in some settings

### Dhar — Journal of Consumer Research, 1997
`Consumer Preference for a No-Choice Option`  
https://doi.org/10.1086/209506

Study frame:
- seven studies;
- tested preference uncertainty and choice deferral/no-choice option;
- reported deferral changes related to attractiveness differences among alternatives and conditions affecting differentiation.

Use in Ch9:
- supports preference uncertainty as **one possible** reason for deferral;
- useful where buyer sees alternatives as similarly attractive or lacks decisive advantage.

Boundary:
- consumer-choice experiments, not professional-service sales;
- no universal claim that «подумаю = alternatives too similar».

## 4. Important correction: classic conflict → deferral claim is not robust enough for a sales law

### Evangelidis, Levav & Simonson — Management Science, 2023
`A Reexamination of the Impact of Decision Conflict on Choice Deferral`  
https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4484

Research frame:
- reexamination of published evidence;
- 40 well-powered direct and conceptual replications;
- total n = 26,703.

Reported conclusion:
- results cast doubt on replicability of prior findings linking experimental decision-conflict manipulations robustly to choice deferral;
- authors call for further research.

Use in Ch9:
- chapter must explicitly reject the simplistic law `harder trade-off / more conflict → more deferral`;
- prevents retrofitting any delayed buyer to a convenient psychology story.

Editorial consequence:
- competing-hypothesis verification is more defensible than one causal taxonomy.

## 5. Hidden/internal decision factors are materially relevant in B2B

### Edelman–LinkedIn 2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report
https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report

Study frame:
- survey of nearly 2,000 management-level professionals globally;
- distinguishes visible and hidden decision-makers/influencers.

Reported current-context finding:
- more than 40% of B2B deals stall due to internal misalignment within buying groups, according to the report;
- hidden buyers can materially influence business purchases even when not primary users or direct sales contacts.

Use in Ch9:
- supports `internal decision / stakeholder alignment` as a real B2B hypothesis when a deal stalls after a seemingly positive meeting;
- helps distinguish seller-side uncertainty from buyer-internal decision structure.

Boundary:
- commercial survey, not neutral peer-reviewed causal evidence;
- B2B context only;
- do not transfer 40% figure to Russian SME/B2C as universal;
- recheck report freshness before master if statistic remains in manuscript.

## 6. Price tension already has its own evidence in Chapter 8

Ch9 must NOT reteach these studies.

## 7. Approved synthesis candidate

### Uncertainty / Objection Diagnostic
Reader-facing Russian name candidate: **Диагностика незавершённого решения**.

Status: **AUTHOR-CREATED SYNTHESIS**, not a validated psychological/sales taxonomy.

### Step 1 — observable signal
Record only what actually happened:
- exact buyer phrase;
- silence/no-response;
- delay;
- internal-review request;
- request for cheaper terms;
- comparison request;
- explicit refusal.

No inferred cause in this field.

### Step 2 — competing hypotheses
Generate only materially plausible alternatives. Candidate hypothesis families:

1. **ЭКОНОМИКА / ФИНАНСОВАЯ ЭКСПОЗИЦИЯ**  
   Confirmed price/budget/payment/risk issue → route to Ch8.

2. **НЕЯСЕН / НЕПОДХОДИТ ОБЪЕКТ ПОКУПКИ**  
   Service result/scope/boundary problem → route to Ch2/Ch7 as appropriate.

3. **НЕДОСТАТОЧНО ОСНОВАНИЙ ВЫБРАТЬ ИСПОЛНИТЕЛЯ**  
   Evidence/provider relevance problem → Ch3.

4. **НЕПОНЯТЕН / НЕПРИЕМЛЕМ ПРОЦЕСС ИЛИ УЧАСТИЕ**  
   Future delivery/role concern → Ch4.

5. **УСЛОВИЯ СДЕЛКИ / СОВМЕСТИМОСТЬ**  
   Known fit/condition conflict → Ch6.

6. **ВРЕМЯ / ПРИОРИТЕТ / STATUS QUO**  
   Buyer has more important use of money/time/attention, switching cost, genuine postponement or rational preference to keep current state.

7. **ВНУТРЕННЕЕ РЕШЕНИЕ / ДРУГОЙ УЧАСТНИК**  
   B2B internal alignment/approval/user/payer/influencer issue.

8. **ПРЕДПОЧТЕНИЕ ДРУГОЙ АЛЬТЕРНАТИВЫ / НАСТОЯЩЕЕ НЕТ**  
   Buyer simply does not choose this provider/service. No repair is owed.

These are **author synthesis**, not scientific taxonomy.

### Step 3 — discriminating evidence
For the top 2–3 hypotheses, ask:
> What observable fact or one neutral question would make one explanation more likely and another less likely?

Examples:
- economic vs provider proof: `Если оставить объём и результат прежними, но изменить график платежей, это решает вопрос или основное сомнение в другом?`
- timing vs internal approval: `Что должно произойти до возвращения к решению: конкретная дата/событие или согласование с кем-то ещё?`
- genuine no: explicit buyer statement or selection of alternative.

No interrogation tree. One good discriminating question may be enough.

### Step 4 — route action
Possible actions:
- clarify one missing fact;
- provide one relevant evidence item;
- correct/rewrite service decision object;
- adjust visible process/role;
- redesign economic terms via Ch8;
- address one known compatibility condition;
- give time until a real event/date;
- include/enable relevant stakeholder where appropriate;
- accept no and stop pursuing.

### Step 5 — stop condition
Stop `handling objections` when:
- buyer gives clear no;
- no consent/basis for further follow-up;
- proposed action cannot change the identified cause;
- the deal is incompatible under Ch6;
- only persuasion pressure remains.

## 8. Critical uniqueness boundary with Chapter 1

Ch1 already owns:
- book-wide Buyer Uncertainty Map;
- early insight that the same phrase may conceal different purchase uncertainties;
- opening scene around `я подумаю`.

Therefore Ch9 MUST NOT:
- open with another `three buyers say я подумаю` scene;
- reteach the whole Ch1 uncertainty map;
- pretend the insight `one phrase can mean different things` is new.

Ch9 contribution is later and procedural:

`post-offer observed signal → competing hypotheses → discriminating evidence → route to one prior tool / wait / accept no`.

Opening must use a different situation, for example:
- a proposal already sent;
- one internal stakeholder forwards a short message;
- seller is deciding whether/how to follow up after a concrete post-offer event.

## 9. Boundary with Chapter 5

Ch5 = pre-offer problem/requirements diagnosis.

Ch9 = post-offer causal diagnosis of a **decision stall**.

Do not rerun Ch5’s seven-field task map. Ch9 uses only one or two discriminating checks tied to the observed hesitation.

## 10. Boundary with Chapter 10

Ch9 = live deal, action now.

Ch10 = post-outcome learning across wins/losses, fact/inference/pattern/test.

Ch9 should not build a win/loss database or infer a recurring pattern from one deal.

## 11. Ethics / anti-manipulation

Chapter must explicitly reject:
- `возражение нужно отработать` as default doctrine;
- treating every no as hidden yes;
- false urgency;
- guilt/shame/fear escalation;
- endless follow-up after clear refusal;
- assuming buyer owes a detailed explanation;
- pretending seller knows buyer psychology from one phrase;
- using B2B stakeholder complexity to circumvent the contact person.

## 12. Russia application

No universal Russian cultural stereotypes.

Use three contexts only where materially different:
- individual / B2C: often one person can decide; simpler hypothesis set;
- owner-led small B2B: owner may be user/payer/decision-maker but real cash flow/priority can dominate;
- multi-stakeholder B2B: internal alignment/approval can be legitimate cause, supported by current commercial research, but seller must not invent hidden stakeholders.

Any direct electronic-marketing/follow-up/privacy/legal detail needs separate current Russian check if materially specific. Not core.

## 13. Research decision

**PASS FOR CHAPTER ADMISSION WITH EXPLICIT SCIENTIFIC HUMILITY.**

Evidence supports:
- decision avoidance/deferral has multiple forms/antecedents;
- status quo can be a genuine decision alternative;
- preference uncertainty can contribute to deferral in some contexts;
- internal alignment/hidden stakeholders can matter in B2B;
- classic conflict→deferral effect is not robust enough to become a universal sales law.

Evidence does NOT support:
- our exact eight hypothesis families as scientific taxonomy;
- inference of buyer psychology from wording alone;
- one objection-handling script;
- universal causal mapping `дорого = X`, `подумаю = Y`, `не сейчас = Z`.
