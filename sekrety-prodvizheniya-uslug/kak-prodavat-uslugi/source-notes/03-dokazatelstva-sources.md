## 1. Ex-ante quality signals in professional services

### Pemer & Skjølsvik — Journal of Business Research, 2019
`The cues that matter: Screening for quality signals in the ex ante phase of buying professional services`  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296319300955  
DOI: 10.1016/j.jbusres.2019.02.005

Study frame:
- interviews with 51 clients of professional services (management consulting context);
- professional-service quality is difficult to evaluate ex ante because of information asymmetry/opacity;
- clients use quality signals in screening;
- authors distinguish qualifying signals and signals of excellence and identify dimensions of ex-ante service quality.

Use in chapter:
- buyers screen providers using observable cues because intrinsic quality is hard to inspect before engagement;
- different signals perform different functions;
- a signal useful for minimum qualification is not automatically evidence of excellence/fit.

Do NOT claim:
- exact taxonomy applies universally to every Russian profession;
- any one signal guarantees service quality;
- their taxonomy = our Evidence Inventory.

## 2. Brand credibility / perceived risk

### Baek & King — Journal of Services Marketing, 2011
`Exploring the consequences of brand credibility in services`  
https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/exploring-the-consequences-of-brand-credibility-in-services/  
DOI: 10.1108/08876041111143096

Study frame:
- survey n=385;
- studied brand credibility, perceived quality, perceived risk, information cost, value for money and purchase intention across several service categories;
- reported credibility effects varied by conditions/service type.

Use:
- credibility can operate as a risk/information-cost signal;
- signal effect is contextual.

Boundary:
- student sample/limited categories noted by authors;
- not support for a generic «build personal brand» chapter.

## 3. Corporate reputation in B2B services

### `Customer perceived value in B-t-B service relationships: Investigating the importance of corporate reputation`
Industrial Marketing Management 37(2), 2008  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0019850107001162  
DOI: 10.1016/j.indmarman.2006.09.001

Use:
- reputation can contribute to customer-perceived value in high-intangibility B2B service relationships;
- supports reputation as shorthand/signal where intrinsic value is difficult to assess.

Boundary:
- reputation is not evidence of assignment-specific fit by itself.
