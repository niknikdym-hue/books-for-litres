## 2. Central reader question

**Как профессионально продвигать конкретный товар или услугу через SMM в российской действительности?**

The book fails if it answers a weaker question such as:
- «в каких соцсетях вести бизнес»;
- «что постить»;
- «как набрать подписчиков»;
- «как составить контент-план»;
- «как пользоваться нейросетями для постов».

Those are downstream tasks. The book must teach the reader to build and operate the social-media layer of a real business.

## 3. Primary reader

Primary reader is defined by responsibility, not job title:

**a Russian business owner, self-employed professional, in-house marketer, B2B marketer or responsible SMM specialist who must decide how social media should contribute to the sale of a specific product or service.**

The same decision system must remain usable across materially different businesses without flattening them into one generic persona.

Primary stress-test contexts:
1. mass/FMCG goods;
2. considered/durable consumer goods;
3. complex/high-ticket B2B goods;
4. local recurring services;
5. expert/professional services;
6. complex B2B services;
7. professional SMM specialist diagnosing a client's model rather than copying a template.

## 4. Reader problem

3. which offer/sales archetype the business belongs to;
4. which Russian-current platforms are usable and economically relevant;
5. what the buyer must remember, understand, trust or verify;
6. what recurring content jobs are required;
7. how organic, paid, creator/community and other distribution modes should combine;
8. where social attention must hand off to checkout, booking, consultation, lead or sales process;
9. what to measure without turning vanity metrics into business truth;
10. how to diagnose failure and choose `STOP | FIX | TEST | SCALE`;
11. where AI adds leverage and where it destroys differentiation, evidence quality or brand memory;
12. what time-sensitive platform/legal facts must be rechecked before execution.

## 5. Central promise

The book gives the reader a **professional decision system for building, launching, operating and improving an SMM operating model for a specific product or service in Russia**.

The reader moves from business economics and buying mechanics to a bounded platform/content/distribution/conversion/measurement system and finishes with a working SMM Operating System for their own business.

This is a course-in-a-book, not a platform encyclopedia and not a collection of tactics.

## 6. Central causal model

Working book mechanism:

`BUSINESS ECONOMICS / GOAL → BUYER & BUYING PROCESS → OFFER / SALES ARCHETYPE → ROLE OF SOCIAL → PLATFORM / AUDIENCE FIT → TRUST & CONTENT JOBS → DISTRIBUTION → CONVERSION / HANDOFF → MEASUREMENT → DIAGNOSIS → ITERATION`

This is an **Elena Dym synthesis and operating model**, not a claim of a newly discovered scientific law.

The essential implication is that platform choice, formats and posting frequency are downstream. A business can execute them perfectly and still fail if an upstream decision is wrong.

## 7. Original contribution / why this book deserves to exist in the AI era

The book does not claim to invent brand growth, service marketing, persuasion, B2B buying or social-media strategy. Its contribution is the integration and decision compression required for a Russian reader in 2026+.

The book must materially outperform a strong generic AI answer on all of these dimensions:

1. **Archetype diagnosis.** It routes the reader by purchase mechanics, risk, buying unit, capacity and handoff rather than swapping industry examples inside one template.
2. **World-view comparison.** It names materially used schools/authors, shows what each explains, shows conflicts and limits, then makes an explicit Elena Dym decision.
3. **Russia-current execution.** It separates platform access, organic use, paid promotion, legal status, audience fit, business fit, measurement and freshness.
4. **Reader-owned artifacts.** It produces a connected operating system, not advice fragments.
5. **Decision rules under uncertainty.** It distinguishes future demand from current demand, leading signals from outcomes and measurement from attribution certainty.
6. **Cross-chapter dependency.** Each major part enables a decision required by the next; chapters cannot be reordered into a generic list without losing the causal sequence.
7. **Author judgment.** The book does not hide behind synthetic consensus or bibliography name-dropping.
8. **Anti-commodity content logic.** AI makes content units cheap; therefore judgment, proof, distinctiveness, buyer insight, distribution and operational feedback become more valuable.

### Current free/course benchmark implication

That confirms that those topics alone are not differentiation. The book must win on **business-archetype routing + evidence-backed synthesis + Russian freshness + a complete operating/diagnostic system**.

## 8. Admitted Offer / Sales Archetypes

The taxonomy is based on buying and selling mechanics, not industries.

### G1 — Mass / FMCG / low-involvement goods

Typical structural conditions:
- frequent/routine purchase;
- low individual decision cost;
- broad category-buyer universe;
- sale often completes outside social media;
- distribution/physical availability is structurally important.

Primary SMM jobs:
- mental availability and salience;
- distinctive brand encoding;
- relevant reach/frequency;
- category-entry-point/occasion linkage;
- creator/cultural participation where useful;
- retail/e-commerce availability cues.

Not default:
- lead generation from every post;
- long educational nurture;
- follower growth as a business outcome.

### G2 — Considered / durable consumer goods

Typical structural conditions:
- longer comparison;
- higher price/risk;
- demonstrations, reviews and objections matter;
- repeated exposures can precede purchase.

Primary SMM jobs:
- pre-demand awareness;
- demonstration/comparison;
- proof and uncertainty reduction;
- qualified handoff to product page, store, configurator or consultation;
- lawful re-engagement where useful.

### G3 — Complex / high-ticket B2B goods

Typical structural conditions:
- rare purchase;
- long sales cycle;
- multiple stakeholders;
- high cost of error;
- technical/application proof;
- sales/engineering/procurement handoff.

Primary SMM jobs:
- mental availability among relevant future buyers;
- expertise/application knowledge;
- credible technical/business proof;
- buyer-role education;
- active + future demand support;
- qualified sales handoff.

The 95/5 framing may be used only as an explanatory B2B principle tied to purchase frequency, not as a universal literal ratio.

### S1 — Local recurring service

Typical structural conditions:
- geography is a hard constraint;
- capacity/slots matter;
- repeat is economically important;
- visible/social proof is often powerful;
- booking is a concrete conversion.

Primary SMM jobs:
- local discovery;
- proof of result/consistency;
- provider trust;
- booking;
- repeat/referral;
- capacity balancing.

### S2 — Expert / professional service

Typical structural conditions:
- quality is difficult to evaluate before purchase;
- expertise/method/fit matter;
- result may be delayed;
- buyer and user may differ;
- consultation/trial/diagnosis can reduce uncertainty.

Primary SMM jobs:
- make expertise legible;
- demonstrate diagnostic judgment/method;
- establish honest proof and fit/non-fit;
- support longer consideration;
- lead to a qualified consultation/trial/application.

### S3 — Complex B2B service — ADMITTED

This archetype is admitted as separate because it combines two mechanisms that cannot be represented cleanly by S2 or G3 alone:
- long-cycle, multi-stakeholder B2B buying and sales handoff;
- service intangibility plus implementation/team/process risk.

Typical examples: consulting, agency, IT implementation and other professional B2B services where the buyer evaluates not only expertise but delivery capability and organizational risk.

Primary SMM jobs:
- category/brand mental availability among relevant buyers;
- executive/expert credibility;
- problem framing and diagnostic authority;
- implementation/case proof;
- stakeholder-specific education;
- sales enablement and qualified handoff;
- future-demand support.

### Not admitted as standalone archetypes at Definition v1.0

Restaurants/hospitality, real estate, education, SaaS, subscriptions and multi-location businesses are **stress-test cases**, not automatic new archetypes. They are routed through the existing decision dimensions unless research at Architecture proves a materially different mechanism that cannot be represented without distortion.

This prevents industry taxonomy explosion and protects book density.

## 10. Final Elena Dym strategic positions for Definition

These positions are approved **inside this proposed Definition candidate only**; they do not become binding book authority until Owner accepts the pack.

### Position 1 — What SMM is

**SMM is not a business goal and not a content factory. It is a managed layer of social attention, memory, trust, proof, interaction and distribution that performs the jobs the business actually needs from social channels.**

Therefore the first question is not «что постить?», but «какую бизнес- и buyer-функцию social должен выполнить здесь?».

### Position 2 — Where planning begins

**SMM planning begins with offer economics + buying process + offer/sales archetype, not with platform, format or demographic targeting.**

### Position 3 — Reach

**Distinguish `CATEGORY REACH` from `OPERABLE REACH`.**

Do not shrink future demand merely because narrow audiences are easier to attribute, but do not pay for reach outside real geography, availability, capacity or economically reachable buying universe.

### Position 4 — Future demand vs current demand

**A professional SMM system explicitly separates work for `FUTURE DEMAND` and `CURRENT DEMAND`.**

The mix depends on category frequency, sales cycle, cash runway and capacity. One invisible metric/content system must not be forced to perform both jobs.

### Position 5 — Content

**Content is designed by JOB, not by rubric, format or posting frequency.**

A recurring content type must perform a defined function: memory, demonstration, proof, education, comparison, objection reduction, community, action, repeat or another justified job.

### Position 6 — Goods vs services

**The decisive SMM difference is what the buyer can verify before paying, what risk remains unverifiable and how the purchase is completed.**

For services, competence, process, fit, experience, capacity and delivery risk are often part of the marketing object itself.

### Position 7 — B2B vs FMCG

**Memory/availability principles may be shared; the operating system is not.**

A mass product and an industrial system differ in buying frequency, buying unit, proof, distribution, sales handoff and appropriate measurement. Calling both «brand awareness» is not enough.

### Position 8 — Platform choice in Russia

**Choose the job and archetype first; choose the platform second.**

Every platform must pass:

`ACCESS → AUDIENCE FIT → JOB / FORMAT FIT → ORGANIC → PAID → LEGAL / COMPLIANCE → HANDOFF → MEASUREMENT → ECONOMICS / PRODUCTION BURDEN → FRESHNESS`.

Large audience does not equal recommendation.

### Position 9 — Measurement

**Measurement exists to improve decisions, not to manufacture certainty.**

Each SMM job needs a leading signal, business-relevant outcome, known limitation and review/decision rule. Deep general attribution and incrementality remain outside this book.

### Position 10 — AI

**AI lowers the scarcity of content units; therefore generic content becomes less valuable. The scarce assets become judgment, distinctive memory, credible evidence, real expertise, distribution, buyer insight and operational feedback.**

AI can accelerate research, variants, production, repurposing and diagnosis but cannot own factual integrity, author/brand voice, customer truth, legal compliance or final judgment.

## 11. Russia platform layer — pre-Architecture disposition as of 2026-09-09

This section is a decision layer, not a permanent list. Every material platform/legal statement is time-sensitive.

### ВКонтакте — CORE CANDIDATE

Why:
- normal access and organic use;
- mature paid route via VK Реклама;
- large current Russian audience;
- business/community/content/video/commerce functions;
- broad archetype applicability.

Current evidence anchor: VK's 13 Aug 2026 H1/Q2 release reports Q2 2026 average monthly VK audience in Russia of 94.3m and average daily 65.2m, with Mediascope as the stated source. These figures are evidence of scale, **not proof of fit for a specific business**.

### Одноклассники — CORE FOR RELEVANT AUDIENCES / CONDITIONAL BY ARCHETYPE

Why:
- normal access and business use;
- paid promotion available through current ecosystem tools;
- materially relevant older-audience usage;
- can be strong for local/service/consumer contexts where audience fit exists.

Current evidence anchor: Mediascope Jan–Jul 2026 reports 40–54 users over-indexing in OK/VK and 55–69 / 70+ in Dzen/OK. VK's Q2 release reports 32.6m average monthly OK users in Russia.

### Дзен — CONDITIONAL CORE

Potential jobs:
- content/discovery;
- deeper explanation;
- relevant older/mid-age audience segments;
- channel promotion through current VK advertising flow.

Do not treat Dzen as a universal social substitute. Fit depends on content job and buying process.

### RUTUBE — VIDEO CORE CANDIDATE / ECONOMICS-CONDITIONAL

Current official RUTUBE business pages confirm paid video advertising and current promotion products. However current package/budget/format details are time-sensitive and can exclude small businesses economically.

Use when video job, audience and economics justify it; do not recommend merely because it is a domestic video platform.

### MAX — EMERGING / TEST, now materially stronger than an experimental placeholder

Current platform evidence has strengthened since the earliest research checkpoint:
- public business channels are available;
- partner tools for Russian entrepreneurs/self-employed are available;
- VK Реклама added channel promotion for MAX;
- VK's 13 Aug 2026 release describes substantial audience/channel growth and broader channel functionality.

Disposition remains `TEST / ARCHETYPE-DEPENDENT`, not universal core, because maturity, user behavior and business conversion evidence still require ongoing observation.

### Telegram — MAJOR ORGANIC / COMMUNITY CANDIDATE; PAID = LEGAL/FRESHNESS GATE

Telegram remains a large information/community environment and may perform organic/community/customer-communication jobs.

Paid/native advertising recommendations require an explicit current legal check. FAS clarification dated 25 Mar 2026 states that a transition period through the end of 2026 applies to responsibility for advertising on Telegram and YouTube while the underlying restricted-resource framework remains relevant. The book must **not** convert that enforcement transition into a blanket statement that advertising is simply lawful or risk-free.

### YouTube — COMPARATIVE / CONDITIONAL

Usage persists but access/practicality and advertising/legal conditions are materially constrained relative to the prior baseline. It may be used for global/historical/video comparison and bounded current cases after a fresh check, but it cannot anchor the default Russian execution playbook.

### TikTok — AUDIENCE / FORMAT BENCHMARK, NOT CORE EXECUTION FOUNDATION

Consumption remains relevant, especially in younger segments, but normal Russian publishing/operating conditions remain constrained. Do not build core reader execution on workaround-dependent use.

### Instagram / Facebook (Meta) — EXCLUDED FROM PRACTICAL PLAYBOOK

Do not recommend as a practical advertising foundation for the Russian reader. May appear only as historical/global comparison where necessary and legally safe.

### Platform safety rule

No exact audience number, tariff, ad format, algorithm claim or legal interpretation survives automatically to final manuscript. Recheck before Architecture when it affects structural channel decisions and again before Literary Master.

This book is not legal advice.
