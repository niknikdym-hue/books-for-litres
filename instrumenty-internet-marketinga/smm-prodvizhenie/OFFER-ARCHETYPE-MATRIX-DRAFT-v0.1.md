# OFFER / SALES ARCHETYPE MATRIX DRAFT v0.1 — «SMM продвижение»

**Status:** DRAFT / RESEARCH-BOUND / NOT OWNER-APPROVED  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** разложить товары и услуги по типам, которые materially меняют SMM-решения. Это рабочая типология, а не готовая глава.

## 1. Principle

Книга не классифицирует бизнесы по отрасли ради удобства. Она классифицирует их по **механике покупки и продажи**.

Два бизнеса относятся к разным SMM-archetypes, если у них materially различаются несколько из следующих параметров:

- purchase frequency;
- consideration length;
- perceived risk / cost of error;
- buyer vs buying unit;
- geography;
- capacity / availability;
- degree of expertise/trust required;
- demonstrability / trialability;
- distribution / physical availability;
- purchase path / sales handoff;
- repeat/retention dynamics;
- proof burden;
- economics and allowable acquisition cost.

Следствие: **Snickers и промышленный станок — не один “товар”. Репетитор и мастер маникюра — не одна “услуга”.**

## 2. Reader self-classification — working decision tree

### Step 1 — What is being sold?

- `GOOD` — покупатель получает преимущественно физический/цифровой продукт;
- `SERVICE` — покупатель приобретает выполнение/экспертизу/опыт/результат процесса;
- `HYBRID` — продукт + materially important service layer.

### Step 2 — How often and how quickly is the purchase made?

- frequent / routine / low consideration;
- occasional / considered;
- rare / long-cycle / high-stakes.

### Step 3 — Who decides?

- one consumer;
- household / parent-child / family pair;
- owner/manager;
- professional buying committee / procurement + users + finance + technical stakeholders.

### Step 4 — What blocks purchase?

- lack of salience/availability;
- lack of differentiation/relevance;
- lack of trust;
- lack of proof;
- price/value uncertainty;
- implementation risk;
- geographic/capacity constraint;
- long timing / no current need.

### Step 5 — How does purchase happen?

- impulse / retail;
- e-commerce/direct checkout;
- marketplace/retailer handoff;
- appointment/booking;
- consultation/trial;
- qualified lead → sales process;
- tender/procurement/complex contract.

### Step 6 — What role can SMM realistically play?

- create/refresh memory and reach;
- demonstrate product/use/result;
- build trust/proof;
- educate/diagnose;
- create community/relationship;
- create/respond to active demand;
- produce lead/appointment;
- support a sales cycle;
- support repeat/advocacy;
- or play only a minor role.

The reader should reach a **playbook family**, not a generic “start a VK group and post 3 times per week” answer.

# PART A — GOODS

## G1. Mass / FMCG / low-involvement product

**Example class:** snack/bar, everyday packaged product, frequently purchased commodity-like consumer good.

### Buying pattern
- frequent or relatively routine;
- low individual decision cost;
- large category-buyer base;
- purchase often happens outside social platform;
- physical availability/distribution strongly constrains sales.

### Likely SMM jobs — hypotheses
- maintain/create mental availability;
- distinctive assets and recognizability;
- broad relevant reach/frequency;
- cultural/entertainment/creator participation where brand-fit exists;
- occasion/category-entry-point linking;
- product launch/promotion support;
- retailer/e-commerce/offline availability handoff.

### What is usually NOT the main job
- turning every post into a lead;
- long educational nurture;
- deep one-to-one trust building for every buyer.

### Content jobs
- distinctive brand encoding;
- usage occasions;
- salience/entertainment;
- product visibility/demonstration;
- social transmission when useful;
- promotion/availability cues.

### Measurement emphasis
- reach/frequency;
- brand/search/availability signals where measurable;
- campaign lift/business outcomes where feasible;
- retail/e-com impact, not engagement rate alone.

### Author-synthesis need
Translate Sharp/Romaniuk/Binet-Field logic into Russia-available social/video platforms without pretending a large-brand budget is available to every FMCG reader.

---

## G2. Considered consumer / durable product

**Example class:** furniture, appliance, higher-ticket electronics, equipment for home, other non-routine purchase.

### Buying pattern
- longer consideration;
- comparison and research;
- higher risk/cost than FMCG;
- proof/demo/reviews matter;
- repeated exposures can matter before purchase.

### Likely SMM jobs
- create awareness before active demand;
- demonstrate use/quality/differences;
- reduce uncertainty and objections;
- social proof/reviews/cases;
- send buyer to e-commerce, store, consultation or configurator;
- re-engage interested audience where lawful/available.

### Content jobs
- demonstrations;
- comparison criteria;
- “how to choose” education without fake neutrality;
- proof/material/quality/process;
- user context;
- objection resolution.

### Conversion path
Usually stronger than “like/follow”: product page, store, callback, message, configurator, offline visit, purchase.

### Measurement emphasis
- qualified site/store actions;
- assisted conversions;
- product consideration signals;
- revenue/margin where available;
- not raw engagement alone.

---

## G3. Complex / high-ticket B2B product

**Example class:** industrial machine, production equipment, enterprise hardware, technical system.

### Buying pattern
- rare purchase;
- long cycle;
- multiple stakeholders;
- high cost of error;
- specifications/application/serviceability matter;
- often only a small fraction of category buyers are actively in-market at a given time.

### Likely SMM jobs
- build mental availability among relevant category buyers;
- demonstrate expertise/application knowledge;
- create credible proof/cases;
- educate multiple stakeholders;
- surface use cases/category entry points;
- support future demand and active sales cycles;
- qualify and hand off interest to sales/engineering.

### What must NOT happen
- copying FMCG “viral content” logic;
- measuring success mainly by follower growth;
- expecting every content unit to create immediate MQLs;
- simplifying technical proof into motivational brand posts.

### Content jobs
- application/problem diagnosis;
- technical/operational proof;
- case evidence;
- economics/TCO/implementation risk where supportable;
- buyer-role-specific content;
- expertise and category education.

### Measurement emphasis
- reach among relevant buying groups where observable;
- high-quality engagement / visits from target accounts;
- influenced opportunities;
- qualified conversations;
- sales-cycle evidence;
- future-demand proxies with explicit uncertainty.

### World evidence to test
LinkedIn B2B Institute + Ehrenberg-Bass B2B: mental availability, out-market buyers, category entry points, brand rejection; use principle, not a universal literal 95/5 ratio.

# PART B — SERVICES

## S1. Local recurring service

**Example class:** manicure / beauty master, local salon, local routine personal service.

### Buying pattern
- geography is hard constraint;
- provider capacity matters;
- repeat cycle can be high;
- visual/social proof often powerful;
- trust/safety/quality matter but decision can be relatively short;
- booking is a concrete conversion.

### Likely SMM jobs
- local discovery/reach;
- demonstrate result and consistency;
- reduce trust uncertainty;
- show availability/booking path;
- stimulate repeat/return;
- referrals/advocacy;
- manage capacity, not just demand.

### Content jobs
- portfolio/result proof;
- process/cleanliness/service standard where relevant;
- person/provider trust;
- local social proof;
- availability/new slots/offers where lawful and useful;
- aftercare/repeat triggers when appropriate.

### Conversion path
social → message/booking/calendar/phone → visit → repeat.

### Measurement emphasis
- booked appointments;
- show-up rate;
- repeat rate;
- revenue/capacity utilization;
- cost per booked/served client;
- local reach quality.

### Critical difference from expert service
The reader may choose largely on local proof, convenience, visible result and recurring experience; long diagnostic education may be unnecessary.

---

## S2. Expert / professional service

**Example class:** tutor, consultant, specialist, professional expert service.

### Buying pattern
- buyer cannot fully evaluate quality before purchase;
- trust/expertise/method matter;
- result may be delayed;
- decision can involve another person (e.g. parent + student);
- proof must be honest and context-aware;
- consultation/trial/diagnosis may be important.

### Likely SMM jobs
- make expertise legible;
- demonstrate diagnostic thinking and method;
- create trust and fit;
- address misconceptions/objections;
- establish proof/reputation;
- lead to consultation/trial/application;
- support longer consideration.

### Content jobs
- diagnostic/educational content that reveals expertise;
- method/process explanation;
- case/proof with appropriate privacy/evidence discipline;
- fit/non-fit guidance;
- objection/risk handling;
- decision support for buyer and user when different.

### Conversion path
social → trust/diagnosis → consultation/trial/message/form → service purchase → result/reputation/referral.

### Measurement emphasis
- qualified consultations/applications;
- conversion to paying client;
- retention/result proxies where appropriate;
- source quality;
- capacity and economics;
- not views/followers alone.

### Critical difference from local recurring service
Expertise and decision confidence may dominate visual convenience; geographic reach may be broad/online; consideration and proof burden may be longer.

---

## S3. Complex B2B service — candidate archetype under research

**Example class:** consulting, agency, IT implementation, professional B2B service.

### Why it may require separate treatment
- buying committee + long cycle like complex B2B product;
- service intangibility + trust/method/process risk;
- proof comes from expertise, cases, team, process and commercial outcomes;
- sales handoff is central.

### Gate
Admit as separate archetype only if research proves the playbook materially differs from both G3 and S2 enough to justify reader-facing complexity.

8. `ORGANIC / PAID / CREATOR / COMMUNITY MIX`;

10. `SALES HANDOFF` where relevant;
11. `REPEAT / RETENTION / ADVOCACY` where relevant;
12. `MEASUREMENT`;
13. `STOP | FIX | TEST | SCALE` diagnostics;
14. `RUSSIA LEGAL / PLATFORM FRESHNESS`;
15. `EXAMPLE / CASE FUNCTION`.

No final archetype may be represented only by a paragraph/example.

# 4. Cross-archetype comparison gate

Architecture must prove that the reader can answer:

- Why would Snickers choose a materially different content/distribution/measurement system than an industrial-machine manufacturer?
- Why would a manicure master choose differently from a tutor?
- Which principles are genuinely shared across all four and therefore belong in evergreen theory?
- Which recommendations branch and therefore must not be stated as universal rules?

If the book cannot answer these cleanly, `ARCHETYPE APPLICABILITY = REWORK`.

# 5. No-padding consequence

Archetypes are not permission to repeat the same chapter five times with swapped examples.

Shared mechanism is explained **once** in the evergreen core. Archetype sections contain only the deltas that materially change:
- decision;
- action;
- proof;
- platform;
- conversion;
- measurement;
- risk.

Repetition under different professions/products = `MERGE | DELETE | REWORK`.
