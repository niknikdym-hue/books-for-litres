# READER / PROBLEM MAP DRAFT v0.1 — «SMM продвижение»

**Status:** DRAFT / RESEARCH-BOUND / NOT OWNER-APPROVED  
**Date:** 2026-09-07

## 1. Primary job-to-be-done

The shared reader is the person who carries responsibility for this decision:

**«Мне нужно профессионально продвигать конкретный товар или услугу через SMM в России. Что именно мне делать, почему, где, для кого, как измерять и как понять, что менять?»**

This job can belong to an owner, self-employed expert, in-house marketer, agency/freelance specialist or B2B marketer. The book should not force them into one demographic persona when the real differentiator is the offer/sales archetype.

## 2. Core expensive/frequent decision problems

### P1. Should this business invest in SMM at all?

**Failure cost:** months of content/agency/ad spend with no strategic role.

Reader needs:
- criteria for when SMM is primary/supporting/minor;
- alternatives/opportunity cost;
- realistic role in the buying process.

Output: `SMM Business Role Brief`.

### P2. Which platforms should be used?

**Failure cost:** fragmented effort, wrong audience, legally risky/unstable channel, inability to maintain quality.

Reader needs:
- platform selection by job + audience + archetype + access/legal/business fit;
- explicit rejection reasons;
- minimum viable platform portfolio.

Output: `Russia Platform Portfolio`.

### P3. What changes because I sell this kind of product/service?

**Failure cost:** generic playbook that mismatches buying reality.

Reader needs:
- self-classification;
- archetype-specific deltas;
- goods/services distinctions;
- sales-cycle/capacity/proof implications.

Output: `Offer/Sales Archetype Diagnosis`.

### P4. What should I actually communicate?

**Failure cost:** generic content, category sameness, audience attention without business effect.

Reader needs:
- message/positioning translation to social;
- buying situations / category entry points where relevant;
- proof/trust architecture;
- content jobs.

Outputs:
- `Trust / Proof Map`;
- `Content Jobs Portfolio`.

### P5. How should content be organized without becoming a content treadmill?

**Failure cost:** constant production cost, filler, burnout, low distinctiveness.

Reader needs:
- content by job, not rubric;
- formats chosen after function/platform;
- production priorities;
- reuse/repurposing rules without semantic repetition;
- AI boundaries.

Output: `Content Operating System`.

### P6. How do organic, paid, creators and community fit together?

**Failure cost:** relying on one distribution mechanism or buying reach for content that cannot work.

Reader needs:
- role of each distribution mode;
- when creator/community is useful;
- how paid changes by current legal/platform availability;
- combination logic.

Output: `Distribution Mix`.

### P7. How does social attention become a business outcome?

**Failure cost:** followers/views without purchase path.

Reader needs:
- CTA by job/archetype;
- checkout/booking/message/consultation/lead/sales handoff;
- friction and qualification;
- repeat/advocacy where relevant.

Output: `Conversion & Handoff Design`.

Boundary: deep conversion/quiz mechanics reserved for «Квиз-маркетинг…».

### P8. What should I measure?

**Failure cost:** vanity metrics, false attribution confidence, wrong optimization.

Reader needs:
- metric tied to SMM job;
- leading vs business outcome;
- known attribution limits;
- enough measurement to make operating decisions.

Output: `SMM Measurement Card`.

Boundary: deep advertising-effectiveness system reserved for «Как оценивать эффективность рекламы».

### P9. Why is SMM not working?

**Failure cost:** random changes and repeated spend.

Reader needs a diagnostic sequence distinguishing:
- wrong role;
- wrong audience/platform;
- weak brand memory/distinctiveness;
- weak proof/trust;
- weak content job;
- insufficient distribution;
- broken conversion/handoff;
- measurement error;
- capacity/economics constraint.

Output: `STOP | FIX | TEST | SCALE Decision Queue`.

### P10. How should AI be used now?

**Failure cost:** content sameness, factual errors, brand dilution, false productivity.

Reader needs:
- operation-level AI use;
- human checkpoints;
- differentiation/evidence risk;
- what not to automate.

Output: `AI Use / Risk Map`.

### P11. What is actually possible in Russia now?

**Failure cost:** advice that cannot be executed or creates compliance risk.

Reader needs:
- Russia platform/access/paid/legal/audience/freshness matrix;
- what to re-check near execution;
- evergreen mechanism vs time-sensitive implementation.

Output: `Freshness Register`.

## 3. Reader subcontexts — stress-test set

The book must be stress-tested against at least:

1. **mass goods marketer** — broad category, retail/e-com distribution;
2. **considered-goods business** — higher involvement/comparison;
3. **complex B2B product marketer** — long cycle / buying committee / sales handoff;
4. **local recurring service owner** — geography/capacity/repeat;
5. **expert service provider** — trust/method/consultation;
6. **professional SMM specialist** — must diagnose a client's model rather than copy a content template.

If a recommendation works for only one context, it must be explicitly branched rather than written as a universal rule.

## 4. Reader progression hypothesis

The book should likely move the reader through these capabilities, not necessarily these chapter titles:

`ROLE → ARCHETYPE → BUYER/JOURNEY → PLATFORM → PROOF/MESSAGE → CONTENT JOBS → DISTRIBUTION → CONVERSION/HANDOFF → MEASUREMENT → DIAGNOSIS/ITERATION → OPERATING SYSTEM`.

Architecture must prove the best sequence after research; this order is not yet approved.

## 5. Anti-generic test

A reader problem is not sufficiently defined if the proposed answer is only:
- “know your audience”;
- “create quality content”;
- “post consistently”;
- “engage followers”;
- “use targeted ads”;
- “test hypotheses”;
- “track analytics”.

Every answer must include a decision rule and either an output or an observable check.

## 6. Open demand/reader research

Before final Definition PASS, validate:
- which of P1–P11 are most costly/frequent across target reader groups;
- whether the book is too broad for one volume at 300–350k characters;
- which reader contexts require explicit tracks vs examples only;
- where professional specialists need deeper treatment than owners;
- what high-quality free/AI resources already solve well;
- where readers most need Russia-current translation.

Until validated: `READER/PROBLEM MAP = DRAFT`.
