# WORLD-CLASS BENCHMARK MAP v0.1 — «SMM продвижение»

**Status:** ACTIVE RESEARCH / PRE-DEFINITION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** identify the strongest relevant world schools, their evidence/limits, and the questions requiring an explicit Elena Dym synthesis. This is not a manuscript chapter and not a ranking of authors.

## 1. Keller / Swaminathan — strategic brand-management lens

**Current anchor:** Kevin Lane Keller & Vanitha Swaminathan, _Strategic Brand Management_, 6th ed., Pearson, 2026.

### Useful for
- positioning and brand meaning;
- customer-based brand equity;
- integrated communications;
- designing short/long-term brand strategy;
- digital communication and AI in a broader brand system.

### What it protects us from
- treating SMM as isolated posting activity;
- optimizing channel metrics while losing brand strategy;
- assuming the platform itself is the strategy.

### Limitation for this book
A strategic brand-management framework does not by itself tell a Russian small business or service provider exactly how to choose among current SMM platforms or how to build a channel operating model.

### Author-synthesis question
How much of SMM should be derived from brand strategy versus direct response / service / sales needs for different archetypes?

## 2. Byron Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass — growth, mental & physical availability

**Current anchors:** Byron Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass research; _How Brands Grow_; current institute materials on mental availability and brand growth.

### Useful for
- reach/category-buyer thinking;
- acquisition vs loyalty-heavy myths;
- mental and physical availability;
- memory structures and buying situations;
- skepticism toward over-targeting and engagement mythology.

### What it protects us from
- treating “community love”, engagement rate or follower loyalty as universal growth engines;
- assuming a brand should speak only to a narrow group of current buyers;
- confusing awareness with mental availability.

### Limitations / debate territory
- applications vary by category, scale and business model;
- social platforms also serve service delivery, community, lead qualification and relationship functions that a pure brand-growth lens does not fully specify;
- small/local/capacity-constrained services cannot simply copy mass-market reach logic.

### Author-synthesis question
When does broad category reach dominate, and when do capacity, geography, expertise or buying-unit structure require a different SMM operating model?

## 3. Jenni Romaniuk / Ehrenberg-Bass — distinctive assets & category entry points

### Useful for
- building recognizable brand assets;
- linking the brand to buying situations / category entry points;
- avoiding generic content that is remembered but not attributed to the brand;
- understanding that social creativity must still build memory structures.

### What it protects us from
- content that performs as entertainment but leaves no brand memory;
- constantly changing visual/verbal identity for platform novelty;
- confusing “interesting content” with useful brand encoding.

### Author-synthesis question
How should distinctive assets and category entry points be adapted to service businesses where the person, process, reputation or local location can itself be part of the brand asset system?

---

## 4. Les Binet / Peter Field / IPA — long vs short effects

### Useful for
- separating short-term activation from long-term brand effects;
- resisting short-term online metrics as the only success definition;
- understanding why fame/reach/creative quality may matter beyond immediate clicks.

### What it protects us from
- declaring SMM “ineffective” because a post did not generate immediate sales;
- over-optimizing all content for direct response;
- using last-click/short-window metrics as the entire business truth.

### Limitations / translation need
- classic evidence comes largely from broader advertising effectiveness, not a Russia-specific social-media operating manual;
- exact budget ratios should never be copied mechanically across businesses/categories.

### Author-synthesis question
How should a reader split SMM jobs between memory-building, active demand capture, relationship/service and sales support for their archetype and economic constraints?

## 5. Keith A. Quesenberry — social-media strategy framework

**Current anchor:** _Social Media Strategy_, 4th ed., 2024.

### Useful for
- treating social media as a planned marketing/advertising/PR system;
- integrating paid/earned/shared/owned logic;
- moving from objectives/audiences to platform/content decisions.

### What it protects us from
- “tips and tricks” structure;
- platform-first planning;
- confusing content execution with strategy.

### Limitation for this book
Global platform options and examples cannot be imported into Russia without access/legal/freshness filtering. The book must also go further on product/service archetype branching and reader-owned practical artifacts.

### Author-synthesis question
What should a Russian reader's minimum decision sequence be before creating any content calendar?

## 6. Tracy L. Tuten — social-media marketing research/practice map

**Current anchor:** _Social Media Marketing_, 5th ed., SAGE, 2023/2024 market edition.

### Useful for
- direct category benchmark on social consumers, networks/group influence, social zones and current practice;
- mapping the breadth of the field so our book does not omit material mechanisms.

### What it protects us from
- defining SMM too narrowly as only content publishing or targeted ads.

### Limitation for this book
A comprehensive textbook can become too broad for a practical Russian reader. We need stronger decision compression and business-archetype routing.

### Author-synthesis question
What is essential for professional action, and what belongs in background/parking rather than the 300–350k-character book?

## 7. Wirtz / Lovelock services-marketing lineage

**Current anchor:** Jochen Wirtz, Christopher Lovelock, Patricia Chew, _Essentials of Services Marketing_, 4th ed., current Pearson edition.

### Useful for
- service-specific customer behavior;
- positioning/service value;
- interaction between marketing, operations and delivery;
- trust/experience/capacity implications.

### What it protects us from
- treating a service like a photographed physical product;
- ignoring that the seller/person/process/availability can be part of the product;
- generating demand beyond delivery capacity without regard to service quality.

### Author-synthesis question
How do we split local recurring service (e.g. manicure) from expert/professional service (e.g. tutor) when trust, geography, repeat cycle, proof and buying decision differ?

## 8. LinkedIn B2B Institute + Ehrenberg-Bass B2B research

**Current anchors:** _How B2B Brands Grow_; John Dawes' 95-5 work; B2B Institute / Ehrenberg-Bass materials.

### Useful for
- long buying cycles and out-market buyers;
- mental availability in B2B;
- buying situations/category entry points;
- warning against lead-gen-only marketing for complex purchases.

### What it protects us from
- treating an industrial machine like an FMCG SKU;
- believing every SMM touch should create an immediate qualified lead;
- ignoring future buyers and buying committees.

### Important nuance
The exact “95/5” ratio is a framing tied to purchase frequency assumptions, not a universal literal constant for every category. Use the principle; verify category economics.

### Author-synthesis question
For high-ticket/complex B2B, what social content and distribution build future consideration, and what signals are appropriate before a buyer is actively in-market?

## 9. Jonah Berger — social transmission / word of mouth

### Useful for
- why ideas/products become discussable/shareable;
- separating social transmission mechanisms from “go viral” folklore.

### What it protects us from
- assuming virality is random magic or purely algorithmic;
- using “shareable content” as a slogan without mechanism.

### Limitation
Shareability is one possible content/distribution job, not the universal objective of SMM.

### Author-synthesis question
When does social transmission materially support the business objective, and when is it a distracting vanity target?

## 10. Robert Cialdini — persuasion mechanisms

### Useful for
- social proof, authority, reciprocity, consistency, liking, scarcity, unity as decision heuristics/mechanisms.

### What it protects us from
- treating trust/proof as vague “make people trust you”.

### Limitation
Principles are not a bag of conversion tricks. Ethical/contextual use is required, and they do not replace offer/category/customer-journey design.

### Author-synthesis question
Which proof/persuasion mechanisms are appropriate for each product/service archetype without manipulation or cliché?

# 11. Major tensions the book must resolve rather than hide

## Reach vs narrow targeting
- Ehrenberg-Bass pushes toward broad category reach / mental availability.
- Local services / capacity-limited businesses / highly specialized B2B may face real economic/geographic constraints.
- **Required Elena Dym synthesis:** distinguish `CATEGORY REACH` from `OPERABLE REACH` and define when narrowing is economic necessity vs lazy targeting habit.

## Brand building vs direct response
- Binet/Field warn against short-term-only optimization.
- many small businesses need cash flow and measurable leads now.
- **Required synthesis:** define a dual operating system that does not sacrifice future demand but respects runway/cash constraints.

## Distinctiveness vs meaningful differentiation
- Ehrenberg-Bass emphasizes distinctiveness and availability.
- positioning/service/business strategy still requires meaningful offer/customer fit.
- **Required synthesis:** distinguish `WHY BUY THE CATEGORY / OFFER` from `HOW REMEMBER/RECOGNIZE THE BRAND`; do not create a false either/or.

## Platform-native tactics vs evergreen strategy
- social textbooks/platform guides contain current tactical patterns.
- Russia platform access/legal status changes quickly.
- **Required synthesis:** put durable decision rules in core; put platform implementation in freshness-controlled application layer.

## Content/community vs distribution
- social discourse often overvalues organic content/community.
- growth evidence stresses reach and availability.
- **Required synthesis:** content quality without distribution is insufficient; distribution without useful/recognizable content is waste. Define role by archetype.

# 12. Preliminary Elena Dym synthesis hypotheses — NOT YET APPROVED

These are working hypotheses to test, not final author positions:

1. **SMM is not a channel list; it is an operating layer between business strategy and social attention.**
2. **The correct starting point is not platform or content. It is offer economics + buying process + archetype.**
3. **Goods and services require different proof systems; mass products, complex B2B and local/expert services require materially different playbooks.**
4. **Reach, memory, trust, proof, demand capture, community and service are different jobs; one metric cannot govern them all.**
5. **The best platform portfolio is the smallest set that can perform the required jobs for reachable buyers under current Russian access/legal/economic constraints.**
6. **A content system should be designed by jobs and evidence needs, not rubrics/frequency folklore.**
7. **AI makes content production cheap, so distinctiveness, evidence, judgment, brand memory and distribution become more important, not less.**
8. **Every SMM system needs an explicit decision loop: `STOP | FIX | TEST | SCALE`, otherwise analytics becomes reporting theatre.**

Every hypothesis must survive research, AI-substitution review and Owner approval before it becomes book authority.
