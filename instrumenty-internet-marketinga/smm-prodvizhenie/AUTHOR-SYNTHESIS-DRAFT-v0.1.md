# AUTHOR SYNTHESIS DRAFT v0.1 — «SMM продвижение»

**Status:** DRAFT / RESEARCH-BOUND / NOT OWNER-APPROVED  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** first explicit Elena Dym positions produced from comparison of world evidence/schools and Russia execution reality. These are hypotheses to challenge before final Book Definition.

## S1. What is SMM actually for?

### World views

- **Keller / Swaminathan:** social/digital communication belongs inside a broader brand-management and integrated communications system.
- **Ehrenberg-Bass / Sharp / Romaniuk:** growth depends heavily on being easy to think of and buy; advertising refreshes/builds memory structures and should not be reduced to loyalty/engagement mythology.
- **Binet / Field:** short-term activation and long-term brand effects differ; over-reliance on short-window digital metrics can damage effectiveness.
- **Quesenberry / Tuten:** social media is a strategic communications/marketing system, not a bag of posting tricks.

### Conflict / gap

These perspectives explain different parts of the system, but the practical SMM market often collapses everything into one of two extremes:

1. “content/community/engagement”; or
2. “lead generation/performance”.

Neither alone answers what SMM should do for different business models.

### Elena Dym — working position

**SMM is not a business goal and not a content factory. It is a managed layer of social attention, memory, trust, proof, interaction and distribution that performs only the jobs the business actually needs from social channels.**

For one business its primary job may be mental availability and broad reach; for another — expertise/proof and sales-cycle support; for a local service — trust, booking and repeat; for another business SMM may deserve only a minor role.

Therefore the first question is never “what should we post?” It is **“what business/buyer job must social channels perform here?”**

### Russia translation

Platform availability and legal status constrain *where* the job can be executed, but do not change the underlying job. If a familiar global platform is unavailable/restricted, we move the mechanism to a viable Russian channel rather than pretending the mechanism disappeared.

### Reader action

Write a one-page `SMM Business Role Brief` with:
- business outcome;
- buyer situation;
- 1–3 jobs social must perform;
- jobs social must NOT perform;
- handoff into the rest of marketing/sales/service.

### Observable check

If the team cannot explain why a channel/content activity exists without naming a platform metric, the role definition is incomplete.

## S2. Where should SMM planning begin?

### World views

- Strategic marketing starts with market/customer/value/positioning, not media tactics.
- Ehrenberg-Bass emphasizes category buyers/buying situations and availability.
- Services marketing emphasizes how service characteristics, operations and customer experience alter marketing decisions.
- B2B evidence emphasizes long cycles, out-market buyers and buying situations.

### Elena Dym — working position

**The correct SMM starting point is `offer economics + buying process + offer/sales archetype`, not platform, content format or target-audience demographics alone.**

Age/gender/interests may help media selection, but they do not tell you:
- whether the purchase is frequent or rare;
- whether one person or a committee decides;
- whether proof must be visual, technical, reputational or experiential;
- whether the real conversion is checkout, appointment, consultation or months-long sales handoff;
- whether extra demand is valuable or impossible to serve.

### Reader action

Classify the business through the Offer/Sales Archetype decision tree before platform selection.

### Observable check

If two radically different businesses receive the same SMM playbook after only changing “target audience”, the diagnosis is too shallow.

## S3. Broad reach or narrow targeting?

### World views

- Ehrenberg-Bass strongly cautions against excessive narrow targeting and emphasizes reaching category buyers / building mental availability.
- Performance practice often pushes narrow targeting to reduce near-term acquisition cost.
- Local/service economics can impose genuine geography/capacity limits.

### Elena Dym — working position

**We should distinguish `CATEGORY REACH` from `OPERABLE REACH`.**

A brand should avoid shrinking its future market merely because current conversions are easier to attribute in a tiny segment. But reach outside the business's actual service geography, delivery capability, product availability or economically reachable buying universe can be waste.

So the decision is not “broad vs targeted”. It is:

1. define the economically and operationally real category/buyer universe;
2. reach as much of that universe as the current job requires;
3. use narrower targeting when the constraint is real, not merely because the ad platform makes it convenient.

### Archetype difference

- FMCG: usually wider category reach is structurally important.
- local manicure service: service radius/capacity legitimately narrows operable reach.
- complex B2B machine: universe may be small in absolute numbers but broad across relevant buying organizations/roles and long time horizons.

### Reader action

Build `Reach Boundary`: who is inside, who is outside, and why — with economic/operational reasons, not demographic habit.

## S4. Brand building or direct response?

### World views

- Binet/Field: long- and short-term effects differ and both matter; short-term metrics can distort decisions.
- B2B research: many buyers are out-market for long periods.
- small businesses often cannot ignore immediate cash flow.

### Elena Dym — working position

**A professional SMM system needs separate jobs for `FUTURE DEMAND` and `CURRENT DEMAND`, but their mix depends on cash runway, category frequency, sales cycle and capacity.**

The mistake is not choosing one ratio instead of another. The mistake is forcing one content/metric system to do both jobs invisibly.

### Archetype difference

- FMCG: memory/reach can dominate, activation happens through retail/promo/distribution.
- considered good: brand memory + proof/comparison + active-demand handoff.
- B2B high-ticket: future-demand memory/expertise matters because active-market windows are rare.
- local service with empty calendar: near-term booking can temporarily dominate, but reputation/repeat remains structural.

### Reader action

Split activity into explicitly named `FUTURE DEMAND` and `CURRENT DEMAND` workstreams and attach different success measures.

## S5. What should content be?

### World views

- social-media literature maps many content/network/community mechanisms;
- Berger offers mechanisms of social transmission;
- Romaniuk/Sharp emphasize memory, buying situations and distinctive brand assets;
- persuasion/service literature stresses proof/trust in uncertain decisions.

### Elena Dym — working position

**Content should be designed by `JOB`, not by rubric or format.**

“Expert post”, “entertainment post”, “Reels”, “poll” are formats/rubrics. A content job is a business/buyer function such as:
- create category/brand memory;
- demonstrate a product;
- prove expertise;
- reduce a specific risk;
- reveal a buying situation;
- answer an objection;
- enable comparison;
- create social proof;
- trigger booking/consultation;
- support repeat/advocacy.

Format comes after job and platform fit.

### Author boundary

Not every content unit needs a direct CTA. But every recurring content type needs a defined system function. “We post because we need regularity” is not enough.

### Reader action

Build a `Content Jobs Portfolio`, then map formats/platforms to each job.

## S6. Goods vs services: the proof problem

### World views

- product marketing can often rely on attributes, demonstration, availability and product experience.
- services marketing emphasizes intangibility, delivery process, customer interaction and difficulty evaluating quality before purchase.
- persuasion/social-proof mechanisms can reduce uncertainty but are not substitutes for real evidence.

### Elena Dym — working position

**The core difference for SMM is not “goods need pretty photos, services need trust”. It is what the buyer can verify before paying and what risk remains unverifiable.**

For goods, proof can often be shifted toward product, specifications, demonstration, availability and comparative use.

For services, the buyer may need to assess:
- competence;
- process;
- reliability;
- fit;
- experience;
- expected outcome;
- person/team;
- capacity/access.

Different services distribute this uncertainty differently. Hence manicure and tutoring require different proof systems.

### Reader action

Create `Trust / Proof Map`: what buyer fears, what can be proven before purchase, how, and what cannot honestly be guaranteed.

## S7. Why B2B machine ≠ Snickers

### World views

- Ehrenberg-Bass B2B work shows mental availability and category-buying principles also matter in B2B.
- B2B Institute/John Dawes framing emphasizes long out-market periods.
- mass consumer brand growth evidence emphasizes broad category reach and availability.

### Elena Dym — working position

**The shared principle is memory/availability across buying situations; the operating system differs because buying frequency, decision unit, proof burden, distribution and sales handoff differ radically.**

Snickers-like SMM may need huge numbers of light buyers to repeatedly notice/retrieve a distinctive brand around consumption occasions.

An industrial-machine supplier may need a far smaller but multi-role buying universe to remember the brand across rare technical/business buying situations, trust its expertise and move into a qualified sales process when timing arrives.

Calling both “brand awareness” hides the important differences.

### Reader action

Map `buying situations + buying roles + proof + handoff` before selecting content/distribution.

## S8. Platform selection in Russia

### World situation

Global SMM literature often assumes broad access to Meta, YouTube, TikTok and other platforms.

### Russia reality

Current Russian access, audience redistribution, advertising law and platform availability differ materially and can change quickly.

### Elena Dym — working position

**Choose a platform only after the job and archetype are known, then pass it through eight filters:**

`ACCESS → ORGANIC USE → PAID PROMOTION → LEGAL/COMPLIANCE → AUDIENCE FIT → ARCHETYPE FIT → MEASUREMENT → FRESHNESS`.

A platform fails the practical recommendation if a critical filter fails, even if it has a large audience.

### Reader action

Build `Russia Platform Portfolio` with explicit `USE / TEST / DO NOT USE / RECHECK` status and reason.

## S9. What should SMM measurement do?

### World views

- Binet/Field: short-window digital metrics can miss long effects.
- brand-science schools emphasize memory/availability measures.
- performance practice emphasizes clicks/leads/conversions.
- attribution is imperfect, especially across channels and long cycles.

### Series boundary

Deep advertising-effectiveness methodology belongs to locked future book «Как оценивать эффективность рекламы».

### Elena Dym — working position

**The SMM book teaches measurement only to the point required for operating decisions.**

Each SMM job gets:
- leading signal;
- business-relevant outcome;
- known measurement limitation;
- decision threshold or review rule.

The purpose is not to prove a mathematically perfect attribution story. It is to decide what to `STOP | FIX | TEST | SCALE` without worshipping vanity metrics.

## S10. AI in SMM

### Current condition

AI makes text/image/video ideation and production cheaper and faster, while platforms increasingly automate media buying/creative optimization.

### Elena Dym — working position

**AI reduces the scarcity of content units; therefore generic content becomes less valuable. The scarce assets become judgment, distinctive memory, credible evidence, real expertise, distribution, buyer insight and operational feedback.**

AI should be used where it improves research, variants, production, repurposing or diagnosis without surrendering:
- factual integrity;
- author/brand voice;
- distinctive assets;
- customer truth;
- legal/platform compliance;
- final human judgment.

### Reader action

For every AI use classify:
`SPEED GAIN | QUALITY GAIN | DIFFERENTIATION RISK | FACT RISK | BRAND RISK | HUMAN CHECK`.
